August 26th, 2025: the Commanders cut Ricky Barber. They brought him back. And on November 4th, 2026, he’s on the 53-man roster, and the middle of Washington’s defensive line is counting on him.

This isn’t a panic move. The last time Washington needed help on the defensive line, this was the guy who stepped in and delivered.

Christmas Day, 2025, against Dallas. His NFL debut. Fifteen snaps, five tackles, and a sack. The sack was on Dak Prescott, a six-yard loss, and he got there by beating Tyler Booker. So the first quarterback he ever took down is one this division loses sleep over.

Now, with Daron Payne and Javon Kinlaw both likely done for the season, the guy Washington cut is suddenly one of the most important players in the building.

Welcome to Commanders Floor. Hail to the Commanders. Let’s go.

Part One: The Road Nobody Handed Him

To understand why Barber is still standing, you have to see how long his road was. Nobody ever handed this guy anything.

He redshirted at Western Kentucky in 2019 and became a productive defensive lineman there. Then he moved on to UCF and kept getting better. He made first-team All-AAC, and in 2024 he had 34 tackles and two and a half sacks.

At his pro day, he weighed 296 pounds and ran the 40-yard dash in 5.2 seconds.

And yet, nobody called his name in the 2025 draft.

Washington signed him as an undrafted free agent on May 8th, 2025, then cut him on August 26th, before the season even started. They brought him back to the practice squad the next day, and he waited.

This season, Washington elevated him in Week 2, and he never got on the field.

So when somebody calls this a “depth signing,” understand what that really means. This was two years of waiting for one opportunity to open up. That’s his résumé.

Part Two: The Crisis in the Middle of the Line

Here’s why Washington needs him more than ever: the middle of its defensive line just got gutted.

Daron Payne had fourteen tackles, a sack, and a forced fumble in six starts this year, in the final year of his four-year, ninety-million-dollar deal. He hurt his foot in practice on October 30th, and it’s reportedly a season-ending injury.

The timing is brutal, because Payne was heating up. His tackle rate went from 1.3 per game early in the season to 3.3 per game over his last five. That’s your best interior player playing his best football, and now he’s likely done for the year.

And because it’s the final year of his contract, his last snap of the season could also be his last snap in a Washington uniform. After nearly a decade here, that’s a tough way for it to end.

Javon Kinlaw went on injured reserve the same day with a knee injury. He had thirteen tackles and a sack this season before the injury.

Dan Quinn says both are likely out for the season.

Here’s the wild part: Washington still beat the Eagles 27–10 on November 1st with both of them out.

So who plays those snaps now? The guy the Commanders cut, twice.

Part Three: What’s Left in the Room

Let’s look at who’s actually left on the interior.

Tim Settle played 42 snaps against the Eagles and had four pressures. He did miss two of his four run-tackle attempts, so there’s still room to clean that up.

Shy Tuttle played 32 snaps and got a sack. He also had six tackles and three run stops, and he looked very comfortable playing nose tackle in Daronte Jones’s four-man fronts.

Those are two players who held up in a big win. But that’s close to the whole rotation now, and it’s getting thin.

The numbers so far are encouraging. Through seven games, Washington has allowed 99.1 rushing yards a game and 3.9 yards a carry. On November 1st, without Payne and Kinlaw, the Eagles ran for only 91 yards. Washington also had five sacks and an interception in that game after falling behind 10–0.

So the floor isn’t gone on this team. But with two starters out, every snap in the middle of that line matters more now.

The Rest of the Depth

Washington also signed Daniel Ekuale to the practice squad. He’s 32 and has played 64 NFL games. He’s a veteran body for emergencies.

So the room is Settle, Tuttle, Barber, and a veteran on the practice squad.

And then there’s Johnny Newton, working his way back. His 21-day practice window opened last week, and Quinn said the arrow is going up on Newton and that he’s very optimistic.

Part Four: Why the Season Isn’t Over

This matters more than a typical depth move, because this season is not over.

Look at the last month. On October 11th, Washington tied the Giants 27–27 in overtime at home. On October 19th, it lost 23–17 in San Francisco on Monday night. Then on November 1st, it beat the Eagles 27–10 on Sunday Night Football, after being down 10–0.

That’s two-four-and-one. And the Giants lead the NFC East at only four-two-and-one. Dallas is three-and-five, and the Eagles are two-and-six.

So a team just two games out of first place just lost its two starting defensive tackles, but it’s not out of it yet. That’s why the unglamorous guy in the middle suddenly matters as much as any other name on the roster.

And once you look at what Barber has actually done, it starts to make sense.

Part Five: Why Barber Fits

Go back to this preseason. In the opener against Miami, Barber led the team in tackles and had a sack.

After that game, Dan Quinn said he liked seeing Barber on the edge and as a pass rusher.

And Barber? He said he’s a competitor and that he belongs here. He also said he’d been waiting for his turn.

He said that in August. Three months later, it’s his turn.

He also admitted at the time that he was in a deep group, and he was right. Washington’s defensive line was loaded in August. Now it isn’t, and the guy who was waiting finally gets his shot.

The Scouting Report

Barber is listed at six-foot-three and 295 pounds, though he measured closer to six-foot-one at his pro day.

Scouts say he wins with power and a quick first step. He’s strong against a single blocker and quick off the snap, which is exactly what you need to push a run play backward or collapse a pocket.

And here’s the most underrated part: there’s no new playbook and no learning curve. He’s been in Washington’s system for two full seasons. He knows the calls, the fronts, and the coaches. He just needed snaps.

Part Six: Let’s Be Realistic

I’m not going to sit here and tell you Ricky Barber is Daron Payne. He isn’t.

But “perfect” doesn’t mean replacing a ninety-million-dollar player. Perfect means coming in and filling exactly the role this team needs.

The depth chart already had him behind Payne, with Tuttle and Settle in the room. His job now is simple: hold the middle until Newton is back.

The Money Makes It Smart

Here’s where the move is really smart.

Payne’s cap hit is about 28 million dollars. Kinlaw’s is about 16 million. Both are on injured reserve.

That’s about 44 million dollars of Washington’s cap sitting on the sideline.

So a practice-squad promotion is the cheapest answer possible, especially when you’re already paying two expensive players who can’t play.

Part Seven: The Trade Deadline Question

Now, the obvious question: does Washington need to trade for a defensive tackle?

The deadline is Tuesday, November 10th, at 4 p.m. Eastern.

So far, no one has reported that Washington is trying to trade for a defensive tackle. All the reporting has been about receivers. Ian Rapoport says Washington could land Colts receiver Josh Downs for a third- or fourth-round pick, and Brian Thomas Jr. is still in play from Jacksonville.

So there’s a real chance the interior group we see right now is what Washington rides with for the rest of the season.

And honestly? I think that’s okay, for three reasons.

One: the run defense held up against Philly without Payne and Kinlaw.

Two: Newton could be back in a couple of weeks.

Three: Washington’s bigger need is still at receiver, where Terry McLaurin is out for the season.

If Peters has limited picks to spend, I’d rather he spend them on a young receiver who helps Jayden Daniels for years than on a rental defensive tackle for nine games.

The Payne Trade That Never Happened

There’s one more angle here, and it stings.

Remember the Payne-for-BTJ idea? Remember the Giants’ reported serious interest in Payne? Remember all the deadline talk about his value?

That’s all gone now. With Payne likely out for the season, there’s no trade market for him. If he leaves in free agency, Washington gets no trade return. At best, there’s a compensatory pick down the road, depending on the league formula.

It’s a reminder of what we said all along: with an expiring asset, the price only drops from here. Sometimes an injury takes the decision out of your hands.

Part Eight: What to Watch Over the Next Three Games

Here’s the schedule, and it’s a big one:

Sunday, November 8th: the Rams at home, 1 p.m. on Fox.
Thursday, November 12th: the Giants, on a short week.
Monday, November 23rd: the Bengals.

Here’s what to watch.

First: Barber’s snap count. He hasn’t played a game this season, so Sunday could be his first real action of 2026. How many snaps he gets tells you how much the staff trusts him.

Second: third down. Quinn said he likes Barber on the edge and as a pass rusher. Watch for him on passing downs, maybe kicking outside in certain packages.

Third: Johnny Newton. His 21-day window opened last week, so Washington has to make a decision on him around November 18th. If Newton comes back healthy, the interior rotation suddenly looks a lot better.

Fourth: the deadline, at 4 p.m. Eastern on November 10th, and what Washington does there.

If those things go right, a guy nobody drafted could be part of the reason this season gets saved.

Part Nine: The First Test Is a Big One

The Rams come to Washington on Sunday at four-and-four, and they love to run the football.

They average 125.5 rushing yards per game. Kyren Williams has 548 rushing yards, fourteenth among 116 running backs. Blake Corum adds 5.2 yards per carry. This is one of the best running games in the league.

So the Rams are a real test for a defensive line missing its two best interior players.

But here’s the good news: the Rams’ tight ends and offensive line are banged up too. Colby Parkinson missed practice with a rib injury. Terrance Ferguson was limited with an ankle injury. Steve Avila, a key offensive lineman, was limited with a hamstring injury. Those are exactly the players who power the Rams’ heavy running formations.

If Barber, Tuttle, and Settle can hold the point of attack against a weakened Rams front, Washington can make Matthew Stafford throw, and a 38-year-old quarterback with an elbow injury and eight interceptions is exactly who you want throwing into this defense.

Part Ten: Why This Is a Win-Win

Think about the downside of this move.

If Barber comes in and doesn’t play well, Washington gave up nothing for him and didn’t cut anyone it really needed. Simple.

If he plays the way he did on Christmas Day, Washington found a rotational defensive lineman for free.

That’s the best kind of move: a free pickup of a player who doesn’t need to learn a new scheme, at a position of real need, for a team still in the race.

And honestly, it’s also a great story. A guy who went undrafted, got cut, got brought back, got elevated without playing, and waited two years for his chance. Then, in the middle of a playoff push, the door opens. Those are the players you root for.

Part Eleven: The Bigger Picture

Let’s zoom out one last time.

This defense has changed a lot in the last three weeks. Trey Amos is back and transforming the secondary. Steve Wilks is on the sideline helping Daronte Jones. The pass rush is heating up, with Odafe Oweh at four sacks and K’Lavon Chaisson at three and a half. Sonny Styles and Frankie Luvu are flying around as blitzers. And now, with Payne and Kinlaw out, Tuttle, Settle, and Barber are holding the middle.

Remember the numbers: after Amos’s return, Washington’s pass defense jumped from 25th and 27th in EPA and success rate to seventh and fifth. Against the Eagles, the defense held Philly scoreless for three straight quarters.

This defense doesn’t need Barber to be a star. It needs him to do his job, so everybody else can do theirs.

Ricky Barber went undrafted in 2025, got cut by Washington, came back to the practice squad, and waited two years. On November 4th, he was promoted to the 53-man roster. In his NFL debut on Christmas Day 2025, he had five tackles and sacked Dak Prescott.
