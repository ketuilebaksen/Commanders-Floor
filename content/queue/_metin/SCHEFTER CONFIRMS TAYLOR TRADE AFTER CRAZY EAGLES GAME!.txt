Jonathan Taylor is on the trade block, and Adam Schefter just put a price tag on him: the Christian McCaffrey package. Four picks.

Ian Rapoport says talks are active. And Adam Peters has already said he’s talked to all 31 other teams in the league.

There’s one detail tying Peters to that exact price that nobody in the DMV is talking about. And after last night’s 27–10 win over the Eagles, the question isn’t hypothetical anymore. It’s Monday morning. Should Peters be on the phone with Indianapolis right now?

Then there’s the 2027 mock draft that had Washington picking fourth overall, and what last night’s win just did to it.

Welcome to Commanders Floor. Hail to the Commanders. Let’s go.

Part One: The Report

Saturday morning, Adam Schefter dropped the bomb, alongside Ian Rapoport: the Indianapolis Colts are now open to trading star running back Jonathan Taylor. They also reported that multiple teams had already reached out, and that the talks are ongoing and active.

Then on Sunday, Schefter laid out the price.

Back in 2022, the San Francisco 49ers traded second-, third-, fourth-, and fifth-round picks to the Carolina Panthers for Christian McCaffrey. According to Schefter, the Colts are seeking a similar package for Taylor.

So: a second, a third, a fourth, and a fifth. Four picks. The McCaffrey package.

And here’s the detail that tells you the Colts are serious about that price. Tom Pelissero reported on Saturday that Indianapolis already turned down an offer that included a 2027 second-round pick plus an additional selection. They want a premium.

Who’s Calling?

According to Schefter and Rapoport, the teams that have inquired include the Denver Broncos, Green Bay Packers, Minnesota Vikings, New York Giants, and Seattle Seahawks. Jordan Schultz also reported calls from a similar group, including the Bengals.

What’s Taylor Worth?

Taylor is 27 years old. He’s a three-time Pro Bowler. He doesn’t have a 100-yard game this season, but he’s hit at least 90 yards in five of his seven games.

And the contract is a big reason teams want him. He’s under contract for 2027 at fifteen million dollars and for 2028 at about 9.46 million. According to Pro Football Rumors, that 2027 base salary becomes guaranteed on February 19th.

That’s not a rental. That’s two more years of an elite running back.

The Complication

There’s a catch on the Indianapolis side. According to the reporting, owner Carlie Irsay-Gordon and Taylor met this week and had an in-depth conversation about the future. She’s said to have a real affinity for him, and the Colts would prefer that he be part of their rebuild.

Taylor himself said he hasn’t asked for a trade and that he’s always proud to wear the horseshoe, though he acknowledged a trade is possible.

So even though the Colts are open to it, this isn’t a fire-sale giveaway like Quenton Nelson. Indianapolis only moves Taylor if somebody pays.

Part Two: What About Washington?

First, I have to tell you something straight up, because on this channel we don’t sell smoke.

Washington is not on any of those lists. It’s not on Schefter’s and Rapoport’s list, and it’s not on Schultz’s. Heavy put it this way: Taylor is a long shot for the Commanders. A long shot, but not impossible.

Here’s why I’m not ruling it out.

One: ESPN has already put Washington on the map. According to Athlon, Jeremy Fowler and Matt Bowen named Washington as a team that fits Taylor. Nobody is talking about a phone call yet, but Fowler and Bowen talk to front offices every single day, and they picked us.

Two: the need. Jacory Croskey-Merritt, Washington’s leading rusher with 239 yards, is on injured reserve. The backfield is Austin Ekeler and whoever’s left. The staff has said it will lean on Ekeler more, and he’s 31 years old.

Three: the money. According to Over the Cap, Washington has 25.5 million dollars in cap space, the sixth-most in the league. Not many teams can absorb Taylor’s contract today without cutting somebody. Washington is one of the few that can.

Four, and this is my favorite: Adam Peters’s own words. On Friday on The Sports Junkies, Peters said Washington has talked to all of the other 31 teams, and that he’s personally talked to a ton of general managers. Indianapolis is one of those 31.

Then he said that if there’s an opportunity to get a good player at a price you think is fair, you can pull the trigger.

A good player at a fair price. Does that sound like anybody who just hit the market? I’m not saying anything, fam. I’m just reading.

Part Three: The Detail Nobody Is Connecting

Here’s the detail nobody in the DMV is talking about.

Do you know who was in the 49ers’ front office in 2022, when San Francisco put together the McCaffrey package?

Adam Peters. He was San Francisco’s assistant general manager.

The exact price the Colts are asking for today was invented in a building Peters worked in. Nobody in this league knows better than Peters whether that kind of package is worth it.

And remember what happened next. In San Francisco, McCaffrey won Offensive Player of the Year the very next season.

Part Four: The Problems

Now the other side, because if I don’t tell you this, I’m selling you smoke.

Problem one: the record. Washington is two-four-and-one after last night. Paying four picks for a running back with that record is what teams do right before everybody gets fired in January.

Problem two: the price is already too high. The Colts just turned down a second-round pick plus another pick. That means my ceiling, which I’ll get to in a second, probably isn’t enough.

Problem three: ownership. Irsay-Gordon would prefer to keep Taylor. It isn’t even a sure thing that Indianapolis sells.

Problem four, and this one worries me most: the Giants. They’re on Schefter’s list of teams that have called. Ralph Vacchiano of Fox also reported that an assistant general manager told him there’s a lot of talk connecting the Giants to Taylor.

Think about that. The Giants lead the NFC East at four-two-and-one, two games ahead of us. And who do we play on Thursday, November 12th, two days after the trade deadline? The Giants.

Picture Jonathan Taylor running through our defense in blue, two days after the deadline. That’s the nightmare.

Part Five: What Last Night’s Win Changes

Before the game, my take was simple: if Washington wins tonight, Peters has to call Indianapolis first thing Monday morning.

Washington won 27–10. It’s Monday morning.

The odds that Taylor ends up in Washington were low before kickoff. I’d have said one in ten. A win over the Eagles, with the first comeback of the season and a defense that held Philly scoreless for three straight quarters, moves that needle. Maybe it’s one in four now. Still not likely, but not crazy.

And the Rams game on November 8th is the next domino. Beat the Rams, and Washington is three-four-and-one on deadline weekend, two games out in a division where Dallas is three-and-five and Philly is two-and-six. At that point, adding a young star stops looking reckless and starts looking smart.

Part Six: My Verdict on Taylor

Here’s exactly what I’d do.

Make the call. Today. Don’t wait for the Rams game, because the Giants won’t wait either.

Not with four picks. My ceiling is a second-round pick and a fourth-round pick. Not a penny more.

Why go that high for a two-four-and-one team? Because Taylor isn’t a rental. He’s 27 and under contract through 2028. He’s exactly the kind of player Peters says he’ll buy: someone young, under contract, that another team is willing to move. Taylor is for Jayden Daniels’s window, not just for this November.

And here’s the honest part: the Colts already turned down a second plus another pick. So my offer might not be enough, and if it isn’t, I walk away. Four picks for a running back, however good, is too much for this roster right now.

But even a losing call has value. If Washington’s phone call pushes the price up for the Giants, or forces New York to overpay, that helps us too. If we don’t get him, the Giants better not get him cheap.

Tell me in the comments: do you pay a second and a fourth for Jonathan Taylor? Yes or no?

Part Seven: The Mock Draft That Had Us at Number Four

Now let’s look at the other side of the coin, because before last night’s game, the conversation in the DMV was very different.

Dean Jones of Riggo’s Rag put out a seven-round 2027 mock draft built on the Pro Football Network simulator. In it, Washington picked fourth overall and took Colin Simmons, the edge rusher out of Texas.

The headline made a point: the mock signals the end for some familiar faces.

According to Jones, Odafe Oweh is part of the long-term plan. But Dorance Armstrong and K’Lavon Chaisson are headed out of contract. On the same list of expiring deals are Charles Omenihu, Deatrich Wise, and Javontae Jean-Baptiste.

Jones also noted that Peters has been reluctant to sell, and that if he does sell before the deadline, the injuries to Terry McLaurin and Daron Payne make things even more complicated.

The Disclaimer

This is a simulator mock. It’s not a report or a rumor, and nobody from the team said anything.

The fourth pick came from Washington’s record at the time, one-four-and-one. After last night’s win, that number has already moved.

But the Mock Says Something Worth Hearing

Look at the list of players on their way out: Armstrong, Chaisson, Omenihu, Wise, Jean-Baptiste. That’s five players on the defensive front with contracts expiring in March. Add Payne, also in his final year, and you’re looking at half a defensive line.

And this pass rush isn’t working even with all of them in the lineup. Washington had eleven sacks through six games, and the team leader, Oweh, had three.

That’s why the mock goes edge rusher at four. Not necessarily because Simmons is the best player in the draft, but because it’s the most obvious hole on the roster.

And here’s a detail that gives me goosebumps: the 2027 NFL Draft is in Washington, on the National Mall. Picture Adam Peters walking up to that stage, at home, with a top pick, and the whole DMV losing its mind.

Part Eight: The Purgatory Problem

Here’s the take that’s going to cost me in the comments, so let me be clear first. I’m never rooting against this team. Never. Last night, I wanted to win by thirty.

But before the game, I said that if Washington lost, I’d want Peters to sell everything that expires in March, Armstrong, Chaisson, Omenihu, Wise, whatever someone will pay for, and chase the highest pick possible.

Because the worst thing that can happen to this franchise isn’t finishing with the fourth pick. It’s finishing six-and-eleven with the twelfth pick, no playoffs, and no elite edge rusher. That’s NFL purgatory, and Washington has lived there way too long.

Now Washington won. So where does that leave us?

It leaves us right at the fork in the road, and the Rams game decides which path we take.

Path one, the buyer: beat the Rams, get to three-four-and-one, and believe in the division. Make a smart move for a young player under contract, like Brian Thomas Jr. or, if the price comes down, Jonathan Taylor. Sell only the rentals that don’t help you win now.

Path two, the seller: lose to the Rams, fall to two-five-and-one, and accept reality. Sell the expiring contracts for whatever Day 2 or Day 3 picks you can get, and build toward a high pick in a draft held in your own backyard.

What you can’t do is stand in the middle. Keeping every expiring veteran, making no moves, and finishing six-and-eleven with the twelfth pick is the worst outcome of all. That’s purgatory, and that’s exactly what I’m afraid of.

Part Nine: The Smart Hybrid Plan

So here’s my actual plan for the next eight days, with both paths in mind.

No matter what happens against the Rams:

Sell the pure rentals that won’t be here in March and aren’t key to winning now. If a contender offers a fourth or better for Chaisson, take it. Armstrong, if anybody will take that contract.
Look for young players under contract. BTJ, a 24-year-old receiver under contract through 2027 with a 2028 option, whom Washington is reportedly interested in according to Pro Football Talk. Taylor, if the Colts come down to a second and a fourth.

If Washington beats the Rams: keep Payne and the core defensive pieces for the playoff push, unless someone offers real value, and make one smart buy.

If Washington loses to the Rams: sell Payne, sell Chaisson, sell anyone on an expiring deal with value, and start building toward April on the National Mall.

What this hybrid plan never does is spend future picks on rentals. Every buy has to be a player who’s still here in 2027 and 2028, because that’s Jayden’s window.
