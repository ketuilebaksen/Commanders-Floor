It's official.

According to Adam Schefter, free-agent wide receiver Brandon Aiyuk is signing with the Washington Commanders' practice squad, per his agent Drew Rosenhaus. And according to ESPN's John Keim, Adam Peters confirmed it on Wednesday.

So yes, Dan Quinn just got a triple dose of great news. A former second-team All-Pro receiver is joining his team. His franchise quarterback came out of the Bengals game without a lingering injury. And his running back came back from injured reserve and scored the only touchdown of Monday night's win.

But on Commanders Floor, I don't just give you the party version. I give you the fine print. What Quinn said, what Peters said, what Kyle Shanahan said in San Francisco, and why I think this signing is really about next year.

Then, an exit almost nobody noticed: a veteran cornerback Washington released before the Bengals game, claimed by the Bears within a day, while Washington's own starting corner is still hurt.

And finally, the most dangerous problem on this team right now. It's not the other team's pass rush. It's the ball coming out of the center's hands.

Welcome to Commanders Floor. Hail to the Commanders. Let's go.

Good News Number One: Brandon Aiyuk Is Coming

Let's start with how fast this moved.

The 49ers released Aiyuk on Monday. He went through waivers on Tuesday, and nobody claimed his four-year, 120-million-dollar contract. That made him a free agent.

On Wednesday, he visited the team facility in Ashburn, worked out, and walked out with a deal. According to Sports Illustrated, the formal signing was expected on Thursday, Thanksgiving Day.

One visit, one day.

And CBS's Matt Zenitz had called it the night before, reporting that things were trending toward the practice squad. He nailed it.

Why This Is Great News for Quinn

Think about where Quinn was a few weeks ago. His receiver room had lost Terry McLaurin for the season. Dyami Brown was on injured reserve. Stefon Diggs was carrying the load at 32, and rookie Antonio Williams was still developing.

Now Quinn adds a receiver who, at his best in 2023, had 75 catches, 1,342 yards, seven touchdowns, and second-team All-Pro honors. And it cost Washington no draft picks and very little money.

That's a huge upside move.

But Here's the Fine Print

There are four details from Wednesday's comments that change what we should expect from Aiyuk this year. And if you're already picturing him lining up on Sunday in Arizona, you might not like where this goes.

Detail One: What Quinn Said

According to Keim, Quinn said it will take a lot of work for Aiyuk to get to the point where he's ready to impact the team in a strong way. Keim added that, per Quinn, there's no timetable for Aiyuk to play.

Quinn also said Aiyuk looked strong and fast in his workout, and that he's excited to add him to the group.

But look at the order: first, "a lot of work." Then, "no timetable."

Washington is 4–5–1, two games behind the Giants, and every Sunday counts. When a head coach in that spot says there's no timetable for a former All-Pro, he's not being modest. He's lowering expectations on purpose.

Detail Two: What Peters Said

Peters was in San Francisco's front office when Aiyuk entered the league. According to ESPN, Peters said Aiyuk had an impact on that team early, and that he really believes in him and thinks he'll help Washington.

But there's another line that stuck with me. According to the San Francisco Chronicle, Peters said on the radio that Aiyuk was a true number one receiver at the time.

"At the time." The general manager who knows him best, choosing his words carefully, was describing the 2023 player, not necessarily today's.

Maybe it's just a figure of speech. I won't pretend otherwise. But Peters doesn't usually waste words.

When asked what he and Aiyuk discussed, Peters said he keeps those conversations private. And he wrapped it up by saying it was a tough situation on both sides, and that it's good for Aiyuk to get a fresh start.

Good News Number Two: Jayden Has Turned the Page

Here's the emotional part.

In July, according to ESPN, Aiyuk went after Jayden on social media, and in one post, he brought up Jayden's mom.

On Wednesday, according to Keim, Jayden responded with real maturity. He said he's a grown man, Aiyuk's a grown man, and things happen. He called Aiyuk a brother and said it's time to put the past behind them.

ESPN's Jeremy Fowler also confirmed the two have reconciled.

Jayden handled this with a ton of class. And that's great news for Quinn, because the biggest potential locker-room problem with this signing is gone.

But Watch One Thing

"Things happen" doesn't mean nothing happened. It's a franchise quarterback saying, "I forgive you. Now produce."

And on-field chemistry doesn't get fixed at a press conference. It gets built through reps.

How many practice reps with Jayden will a practice-squad receiver get, when his own head coach says he still needs a lot of work? Nobody answered that question on Wednesday.

Detail Four: The Money and San Francisco

Here's a detail almost nobody is talking about.

According to the AP, the 49ers voided more than 26 million dollars of Aiyuk's 2026 contract because he missed meetings and team activities during his rehab. Jeremy Fowler reported that Aiyuk has been trying to sort out money he still owes San Francisco.

And Kyle Shanahan, who coached Aiyuk his entire career, said, according to USA Today, that things officially ended for him the last time he tried to reach Aiyuk and couldn't.

Read that again. Peters says Aiyuk's work ethic is beyond dispute. Shanahan says he couldn't reach him.

Both things can be true. There's the Aiyuk of 2020 through 2023, and the Aiyuk of the rehab. Washington is betting the first one comes back.

My Take: This Is a 2027 Signing

I know this isn't the popular take in the DMV right now.

In the last video, I argued Washington should offer Aiyuk the 53-man roster instead of the practice squad. But after hearing what Quinn, Peters, and Shanahan said, I've changed my mind.

This signing is for 2027, not 2026. And the practice squad is exactly the right call.

Look at the facts:

Aiyuk hasn't played an NFL game since October 20th, 2024, against Kansas City, when he tore the ACL, MCL, and meniscus in his right knee.
He passed his physical on November 9th.
Quinn says there's no timetable.
Peters said "at the time."
Shanahan says he couldn't reach him.

With all of that on the table, the practice squad gives Washington exactly what it needs.

Washington can watch him practice every day. He doesn't take up a 53-man roster spot. No source has reported a deal running past this season, so as far as we know, Washington hasn't committed anything for next year.

If he's ready sooner than expected, they call him up. If he's never ready this year, they lost almost nothing.

The Real Play Is in March

By March, Aiyuk will already be inside the building. He'll know the playbook. He'll have practiced with Jayden. And the medical staff will have watched him up close for weeks.

Washington will be negotiating with a player it knows from the inside, not one who walks in from the outside with a medical report and a smile. That's worth more than 15 snaps in December.

My Prediction

Aiyuk doesn't play a snap before mid-December. And if he plays at all the rest of the year, it'll be in a small role. Anyone expecting him to start in January is going to be disappointed.

Could I be wrong? Of course. If he gets called up in two weeks and drops 80 yards on somebody, come back to this video and laugh at me.

But going by what the people who know him said this week, this is a bet on the future.

Good News Number Three: Bill Is Back

Now the third piece of good news.

Jacory Croskey-Merritt, whom the team calls Bill, came off injured reserve for Monday's game against the Bengals. And according to Quinn's postgame press conference, Bill scored Washington's only touchdown, at the end of the first half.

Think about that. Croskey-Merritt missed four games: the 49ers, the Eagles, the Rams, and the Giants. In his first game back, against a 7–2 Bengals team, he scored the only touchdown in a two-point win.

That's a huge return, and it gives Quinn a real early-down runner again for the stretch run.

Jayden's Health

There's also good news on Jayden.

According to Sports Illustrated, Quinn said Jayden came out of the Bengals game banged up on his non-throwing side. But Quinn said it was not lingering, and that it didn't limit him.

The hit came on a scramble that went out of bounds, not on a bad snap. I want to be clear about that.

So Jayden's okay. But keep that in mind, because it connects to the third concern later in this video.

The Concerns: Isaac Yiadom's Exit

While one player came in through the front door with cameras rolling, another walked out the back without anyone noticing. And for Sunday's game, that move might matter more.

Before Monday's game against Cincinnati, Washington needed a 53-man roster spot to activate Croskey-Merritt from injured reserve. According to SI, Washington released cornerback Isaac Yiadom.

According to Bears Wire, Yiadom hit waivers on Monday. On Tuesday afternoon, Adam Schefter posted the news: the Bears had claimed him. According to Heavy, the NFL's official transaction wire lists it on Tuesday, November 24th.

End of story for most people. A depth corner who barely played heads to another team. I understand that reaction. But the details are where it gets interesting.

Who Yiadom Is

Yiadom was a third-round pick in the 2018 draft. According to Heavy, Chicago is the eighth team of his career, not counting two separate stints in New Orleans.

But he's not a throw-in, even if that number makes him look like one.

In 2023 with the Saints, according to Heavy, he started eight of 17 games and allowed a 48.9 percent completion rate in coverage, the lowest of his career. In 2023 and 2024, he played all 17 games each season and logged more than a thousand defensive snaps across those two years.

In Washington, he went through the practice squad and was called up to the active roster in September. According to the Chicago Sun-Times, he played zero defensive snaps in three games, plus 55 special teams snaps.

Chicago's Situation

Now look at Chicago.

According to the Sun-Times, Bears cornerback Kyler Gordon is still out with a shoulder injury, and safety Elijah Hicks has been ruled out for Thursday with a concussion. And on Thursday, the Bears play in Detroit on Thanksgiving.

According to Heavy, Yiadom will most likely be asked to take Hicks's role on special teams. And the Bears had an open roster spot, so they didn't even have to cut anyone.

In other words, a team with injuries in its secondary saw a veteran corner on the waiver wire and didn't wait a single day.

Washington's Secondary

Now look at Washington's secondary.

Amik Robertson missed Monday's game with an ankle injury. According to SI, Rasul Douglas and Trey Amos played all 58 defensive snaps against the Bengals. Fabian Moreau played 10, and Mike Sainristil played four.

And according to the Wednesday injury report published by Hogs Haven, Robertson didn't practice because of the ankle. If he can't play Sunday, that's two straight missed games.

What Trey Amos's Snap Count Tells You

Look at one detail in those numbers. Trey Amos played 58 of 58 snaps against Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins. That's a huge night for a young cornerback, and he deserves credit.

But it also tells you the staff didn't have anyone it trusted enough to give him a breather.

When a young player never leaves the field, it's not always because he's untouchable. Sometimes it's because the next player on the depth chart doesn't convince anyone.

My Take

So in the same week, Washington's starting corner has a bad ankle, and the team lets a veteran corner go. And he doesn't disappear. Another NFC team claims him within a day.

I think Washington cut the wrong player in the wrong week.

The Other Side

I understand the other side, and it's a good argument. Yiadom wasn't playing a single defensive snap. Croskey-Merritt came back and scored the game's only touchdown. The move worked on the scoreboard.

If you have to choose between a corner who doesn't play and a running back who scores a touchdown, you pick the running back.

All of that is true. But the question isn't Bill or Yiadom. The question is why the roster spot had to come out of the secondary, with Robertson hurt, a young corner playing every snap, and Moreau coming in for only ten.

The secondary was the last position group I'd take a body away from. There were other spots on the 53-man roster. I won't make up a name, because I don't know who was next in line. But the principle is simple: you don't cut depth from a group that's banged up.

Special Teams Matter Too

And the special-teams part isn't a footnote. Yiadom played 55 special-teams snaps in three games.

On Monday night, Washington won because of a rookie kicker who went five for five. Special teams win and lose games. And Chicago figured that out in a day.

The Worst Case

The worst case: Robertson doesn't play in Arizona, Moreau's snaps go way up, and by December, Washington is out on the street looking for a veteran corner like the one it just gave away.

Could I be wrong? Yes. If Robertson is back on Sunday and the secondary plays well, this becomes a minor move, and I'll look like I'm overreacting.

But good front offices don't wait for the worst to happen before building depth. And this time, Chicago was faster than Washington.

The Concerns: The Center Problem

If the secondary worries me, on offense there's a smaller problem that's even more dangerous. It happens on every single drive, starting with the first play.

There are no fireworks in this story, and that's exactly why it worries me.

According to SI, Lucas Patrick played all 68 offensive snaps at center against the Bengals.

And SI's Philip Hughes wrote that one thing stood out the whole game: when Washington was in shotgun, Patrick's snaps had Jayden reaching all over the place.

And it's not just one reporter's opinion. On Tuesday, Quinn was asked about it, and according to SI, he said the snaps were inconsistent in both location and speed.

When a head coach says that publicly about his own center, it's because he saw it on film, and he saw it a lot.

Why It Matters So Much

Think about it play by play.

In shotgun, Jayden gets the ball several yards behind the center, and in that same second, he has to read the defense. Where's the safety? Is a blitz coming? Is the linebacker dropping into coverage?

If the ball comes in high, low, off to the side, or too slow, Jayden loses his eyes. He's looking at the ball instead of the defense.

In the NFL, half a second is the difference between a completion and a sack, between reading the blitz and eating it.

Connecting to Jayden's Injury

Remember, Quinn said Jayden got banged up on his non-throwing side on Monday. That hit came on a scramble, not a bad snap.

But the principle is the same. Every time Jayden has to improvise, he's exposed. And a bad snap is the most avoidable way to force him to improvise.

The Background

If you haven't followed this story, here's the background.

Nick Allegretti, according to Commanders Wire, played 100 percent of the snaps at center over the first three weeks of the season before moving to guard.

In early November, Patrick suffered a concussion, and sixth-round rookie Matt Goldman came in and, according to Athlon, graded well in pass protection on his snaps per PFF.

Then, against the Giants, Allegretti played center and, according to PFF, allowed just one pressure, with much cleaner snaps.

When Patrick returned for the Bengals game, Washington went back to him at center.

To Be Fair to Patrick

To be fair, Patrick has been solid in pass protection. According to SI back in October, he had played 258 snaps at center without allowing a sack.

So he can block. The issue is the snap.

My Take

So here it is, straight up: if Patrick doesn't fix his snapping in practice this week, Allegretti has to be the center on Sunday in Arizona.

I know Allegretti already had his shot early in the season, and the staff moved him to guard for a reason. I know changing centers mid-season can scramble communication on the offensive line.

But Allegretti just proved against the Giants, one of the most blitz-heavy defenses in the league, that he can handle the job with clean snaps. And clean snaps are the foundation of everything Jayden does.

Good news number one: Brandon Aiyuk is signing with Washington's practice squad, confirmed by Adam Peters, according to ESPN. A former second-team All-Pro, added at almost no cost.
