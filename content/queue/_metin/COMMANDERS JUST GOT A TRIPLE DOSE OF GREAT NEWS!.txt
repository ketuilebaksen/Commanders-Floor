Everywhere you look, everybody tells you the Rams are going to win on Sunday. Every NFL analyst is picking Los Angeles.

But I actually think Washington has a pretty good matchup against the Rams. And this week, the Commanders got a triple dose of good news that backs that up.

Good news number one: the betting line just moved in Washington’s direction.

Good news number two: the Rams’ secondary is banged up, and an old friend could be forced into a big role.

Good news number three: Washington’s run defense and pass rush are heating up at exactly the right time, with Steve Wilks on the sideline.

This is the last game before the trade deadline, and it could decide what Adam Peters does next. Let’s dive into it. Welcome to Commanders Floor. Hail to the Commanders. Let’s go.

Part One: The Line Is Moving

Let’s start with something that caught my eye.

The Rams were favored all week, and the line opened at five and a half points. On Thursday, it moved down to four and a half. A full point.

Why does that matter? A line doesn’t move a full point by accident. It moves because money is coming in on one side, or because the people setting it see something changing, like injuries.

In this case, it’s probably both. People are starting to believe Washington can win this game, and the Rams’ injury report, which we’ll get to in a minute, is a big reason why.

Washington is coming off its most complete win of the season, a 27–10 win over the Eagles, scoring 27 straight points. The momentum is real, and the market is starting to notice.

Part Two: The Offense Found Its Explosives

Before we get to the Rams’ injuries, let’s look at why Washington’s offense has a real chance on Sunday.

Against the Eagles, Jayden Daniels spread the ball around without Terry McLaurin:

Stefon Diggs: three catches, 38 yards, and a touchdown.
Antonio Williams: five catches for 54 yards.
Treylon Burks: three catches for 81 yards, including a 54-yard bomb past a first-team All-Pro corner.
Chig Okonkwo: two catches for 32 yards.

When Jayden completes passes to all of his receivers, he’s usually at his best, and this offense flows.

And here’s the key: David Blough was able to generate explosive plays on Sunday night. That’s what had been missing. The shot plays, the big gains, the long completions that flip the field.

If Washington can do that against the Rams, it has a good shot to win. Get the explosives going, spread the ball to every receiver, and keep the defense guessing.

And there’s a very good chance that happens this Sunday, which brings us to good news number one on the injury front.

Part Three: The Rams’ Secondary Is Banged Up

Look at the Rams’ injury report, and it jumps off the page.

Kam Curl, their safety, didn’t practice on Wednesday with a foot injury.

Trent McDuffie, their top corner, didn’t practice on Wednesday with a back injury.

Jaylen Watson, another corner, has been ruled out for the season with a shoulder injury.

This Rams team is a lot like the Commanders when it comes to injuries. They have a lot of players banged up, and specifically in the secondary.

The Old Friend: Emmanuel Forbes

Do you know who Washington could be facing on Sunday? Emmanuel Forbes.

Our old friend. The former first-round pick who struggled here in Washington. It didn’t work out, and the Commanders eventually released him. The Rams picked him up, and he’s been with them ever since.

If McDuffie or Curl can’t go, or are limited, Forbes may have to step into a bigger role in this game. His confidence might not be sky-high, and Washington’s receivers know him.

Now, I’m not saying McDuffie or Curl won’t play. They might. But they’re clearly banged up. And here’s another factor: the Rams are playing in Week 9 without a bye yet. That’s a lot of football on tired, beaten-up bodies. Washington, meanwhile, is coming off its bye just two weeks ago.

Why This Matters for Washington’s Offense

If the Rams’ secondary is short-handed, this is exactly the week for Blough to keep calling shot plays.

Think about it. Burks just beat a first-team All-Pro corner deep. Williams is always open. Diggs knows how to work against anybody. If one or two of the Rams’ best defensive backs are out or limited, Washington’s receivers will have matchups they can win.

If Washington can take advantage of a beaten-up Rams secondary, it’s going to play a massive role in this game.

Part Four: The Rams’ Offense Lives on the Run

Now let’s flip it to the other side of the ball.

Matthew Stafford is on the injury report too, with a right elbow injury. He practiced fully, and he’s obviously going to play. But Stafford is 38 years old and has been banged up this year.

Here’s the pattern with this Rams offense: when it isn’t running the football effectively, it usually doesn’t play well. Even in last week’s win over the Chargers, the Rams’ offense wasn’t great.

The Rams’ running game is one of the best in the league:

Kyren Williams: 112 carries, 548 yards, 4.9 yards per carry, and six touchdowns.
Blake Corum: 75 carries, 389 yards, 5.2 yards per carry, and three touchdowns. He’s been excellent coming out of Michigan.

When those two get going, the Rams’ offense looks very dangerous. Stafford gets to work off play-action, and everything opens up.

Part Five: But Washington Doesn’t Let Up Yards on the Ground

Here’s good news number two: Washington’s run defense has been one of the most consistent things about this team.

Look at the list of running backs Washington has faced:

Saquon Barkley in Week 1: held to around 80 yards.
Jonathan Taylor in London in Week 4: held to about 95 yards.
Christian McCaffrey against the 49ers: sixteen carries for 64 yards.
Saquon Barkley again on Sunday: thirteen carries for 30 yards, though he was clearly banged up.

Washington hasn’t allowed a single 100-yard rusher this season. Not one. And that’s against some of the best backs in the league.

Without Payne and Kinlaw

The question everybody has is whether the run defense can hold up without Daron Payne and Javon Kinlaw.

On Sunday, it did. Shy Tuttle stepped in alongside Tim Settle and played nose tackle. He had six tackles, three run stops, and a sack in just 32 snaps. Daronte Jones also adjusted the structure, using more four-man fronts instead of the five-man looks Washington had been using.

I really like Tuttle at nose tackle. Even without their top two interior guys, Washington has players in place who can stop the run if the game plan is right.

The Formula

So here’s how Washington wins this game on defense:

Shut down Kyren Williams and Blake Corum. Force the Rams to become one-dimensional.

Make Matthew Stafford throw. He has eight interceptions on the year, and he’s 38 with an elbow injury. The more he has to throw, the more chances Washington has.

Force turnovers. How do you beat an opponent that looks better on paper? You take the ball away.

Remember what happened earlier this season against the Seattle Seahawks: Washington forced three turnovers that turned into 21 points. And Washington entered last week second in the league in turnover differential, at plus-six.

If Washington can intercept Stafford, blitz him, and turn the Rams over a couple of times, it has a real chance to pull off the upset.

Part Six: The Pass Rush Is Heating Up

Here’s good news number three, and it might be the biggest.

The pass rush is starting to come alive.

Odafe Oweh now has four sacks on the year. K’Lavon Chaisson has three and a half. And they’re producing without Payne and Kinlaw in the lineup.

Then there are the linebackers. Sonny Styles, the seventh overall pick, and Frankie Luvu are both fast, aggressive blitzers. When Daronte Jones’s scheme gets them going, they become a nightmare for offensive lines. Luvu was flying all over the field on Sunday.

Jones talked about this at his press conference on Thursday. In my words, he said last week’s pass rush gave a snapshot of what happens when guys work in unison, whether it’s a four-man or five-man rush, and stay detailed in their roles. When that happens, it opens things up for everybody else.

If this Daronte Jones scheme can really get going, watch out. Styles and Luvu as blitzers, Oweh and Chaisson off the edge, Amos and Robertson in coverage. This defense has the pieces.

Part Seven: The Steve Wilks Effect

Jones also talked about having Steve Wilks on the sideline on Sunday, and his answer was interesting.

He said, in essence, that it could take some things off his plate, so he can focus on the next call.

That’s a very different tone from the “probation” talk we heard last week. Jones is framing Wilks as help, not a threat. And honestly, from what people in the stadium saw on Sunday, Wilks was very involved. He was talking to players as soon as they came off the field, headset on, coaching up the defense between series.

My read is that players are going to listen closely to a former head coach and Super Bowl defensive coordinator. Wilks commands respect. And the results, at least for one game, were there: Washington held the Eagles scoreless for the final three quarters.

The Rams are a much better test than the Eagles. But if the Wilks effect is real, Sunday is where we’ll see it.

Part Eight: Defending Puka Nacua

The biggest challenge on Sunday is Puka Nacua.

Jones talked about him at the podium, saying Nacua is really dangerous once he has the ball in his hands. Everybody knows that.

Nacua has dealt with injuries this year, but when he’s active and gets going, the Rams are a very hard offense to stop.

Here’s the interesting part. Jones was asked why he doesn’t have one cornerback shadow the opponent’s best receiver all game. His answer was that someone like Nacua moves around the formation so much that following him would put a defensive back in spaces he isn’t used to.

So Washington won’t just have one corner chase Nacua around the field. You’ll see Amik Robertson on him at times, Trey Amos on him at times, and the defensive backs moving based on where Nacua lines up.

That strategy has worked at times this year, and with Amos back, it has a better chance of working. Remember, Washington’s pass defense jumped from 25th and 27th in EPA and success rate to seventh and fifth in the two games Amos has played.

So here’s the recipe: stop Nacua after the catch, stop the run, and turn Stafford over. Do those three things, and Washington wins.

Part Nine: What David Blough Said

David Blough also spoke to the media this week, and a few things stood out.

On the offensive line: he was asked about the line’s alignment last week, and he declined to comment. Everybody knows this line needs to be better. It needs to take accountability for the penalties, and it needs to open more running lanes, especially with the running back room as thin as it is, with Jacory Croskey-Merritt on injured reserve.

On Cliff Kingsbury: Blough said Kingsbury gave him so much in their two years together, and that he’s incredibly grateful for his time under him. Blough still keeps in touch with him. Kingsbury now works on the Rams’ staff, so there’s a coaching connection on both sidelines.

On Matthew Stafford: Blough and Stafford are former teammates. Blough said he considers Stafford a great friend, but this week, they want to get him. That’s the competitor in Blough. He loves to win and hates to lose.

On the Rams’ pass rush: Blough said the Rams have a lot of really good rushers, with Byron Young getting after the quarterback at an elite rate, and that you can’t just double-team one guy all game. He’s right. The Rams’ defense is very good up front.

That’s why the offensive line has to play its best game of the season. If the Rams’ pass rush gets home, the shot plays won’t matter.

Part Ten: The Full Rams Injury Report

Let’s go through the rest of the Rams’ injury report, because there’s more good news for Washington.

Joe Cardona, long snapper, ankle, did not practice. He’s not a huge on-field factor, but it’s worth watching.
Kam Curl, safety, foot, did not practice.
Trent McDuffie, cornerback, back, did not practice.
Colby Parkinson, tight end, rib, did not practice. That’s a big one, because the Rams use a lot of 13 personnel, with three tight ends on the field. If Parkinson is out, that changes their run-heavy packages.
Terrance Ferguson, tight end, ankle, limited. Another tight end, and another hit to those heavy formations.
Steve Avila, offensive lineman, hamstring, limited. A hit to their run game up front.
Jaylen Watson, cornerback, out for the season.

Put that together: the Rams’ secondary is banged up, their tight end group is banged up, and an offensive lineman is limited. The tight ends and the offensive line are exactly what power that elite running game.

If Parkinson, Ferguson, and Avila are limited or out, the Rams’ run game, the engine of their offense, could be weaker than usual. That plays right into Washington’s biggest strength on defense.

Part Eleven: Why This Game Matters So Much

Let’s zoom out, because this game matters well beyond Sunday.

This is the last game before the November 10th trade deadline.

Win, and Washington is three-four-and-one, two games out in an NFC East where Dallas is three-and-five and Philly is two-and-six. Adam Peters, who has talked to all 31 teams and sounds like a buyer, gets the green light to add a young receiver. Ian Rapoport says Washington could land Josh Downs for a third- or fourth-round pick, and Brian Thomas Jr. is still in play.

Lose, and Washington is two-five-and-one. The conversation flips to selling one-year players for Day 3 picks.

So Sunday decides more than one game. It decides the direction of the season.

Part Twelve: My Prediction

I think Washington wins this game.

Not because the Rams are bad. They’re a good team. But look at the matchup:

A banged-up Rams secondary against a Washington receiver room that just found its explosives.
A Rams offense that lives on the run, against a Washington run defense that hasn’t allowed a 100-yard rusher all season.
A 38-year-old quarterback with an elbow injury and eight interceptions, against a defense that thrives on turnovers.
A Rams team that hasn’t had a bye, playing a Washington team two weeks removed from rest.
A betting line already moving toward Washington.

And Washington has the confidence of coming off its best win of the season.

My keys to winning:

One: stop Kyren Williams and Blake Corum. Make the Rams one-dimensional.

Two: win the turnover battle. At least two takeaways.

Three: keep calling shot plays against a beaten-up secondary.

Four: no pre-snap penalties. The offensive line has to be clean against an elite pass rush.

Five: start faster. You can’t spot the Rams ten points the way Washington spotted Philly.
