Five straight weeks of at least 31 points.

That’s the number hanging over Dan Quinn’s preparation for Monday night, and it came up in his press conference as the Washington Commanders get ready for the Cincinnati Bengals.

Quinn didn’t dodge it. Over a long session, he talked about how to attack a team that’s been good against everything, how Jayden Daniels learned to take his shots, how he wants to feature his receivers, and what’s changed about a defense that was giving up points in bunches earlier this season.

And there was real news. A key offensive lineman is back. A running back’s return is still on the table for Monday. And Quinn made it clear he wants the ball in Antonio Williams’s hands more.

There’s a lot in here, and I want to go through all of it, because Quinn’s answers show exactly how he’s thinking about this game.

Welcome to Commanders Floor. Hail to the Commanders. Let’s go.

Part One: The Injury News

Let’s start with what you probably care about most: the injury updates.

Lucas Patrick Is Back

Asked about Lucas Patrick’s status, Quinn said he’s good to go and was back at practice.

That’s significant. Patrick had been in the concussion protocol since the Rams game. Now he’s cleared, which brings us right back to the offensive line decision we talked about earlier this week.

Remember the situation. Nick Allegretti played very well at center against the Giants. According to Pro Football Focus, he allowed just one pressure all game against one of the most blitz-heavy defenses in the league. And his snaps were much cleaner than Patrick’s, who had been sending high snaps on ten or more passing plays per game.

So now Quinn has a real decision:

Option one: Patrick goes back to center.
Option two: Allegretti stays at center, and Patrick moves to left guard.
Option three: Allegretti stays at center, and Patrick becomes depth.

Quinn didn’t say which way he’s leaning, but Patrick being healthy gives him options he didn’t have a week ago.

Bill Croskey-Merritt: Not Ruled Out

Next, Jacory Croskey-Merritt, whom Quinn and the team call Bill.

A reporter asked whether his return was more likely to come in a later week than this one. Quinn said Croskey-Merritt has worked hard, and the team wants to go through the full week with him. That’s why the team opened his practice window: to have him go through every practice this week.

Quinn said they’re definitely not ruling him out for Monday’s game. He said Croskey-Merritt is strong and fit and looks good. But for Quinn, the football part is always the biggest piece, and he wants to see how Croskey-Merritt performs over the next few days of practice.

He said he likes where they’re starting.

So it’s not a guarantee, but it’s a real possibility. And for an offense that’s been searching for a consistent run game, getting Croskey-Merritt back would be a big boost.

Remember, before he went on injured reserve, Croskey-Merritt was the lead back. Since then, Rachaad White and Austin Ekeler have carried the load. White has averaged a little over four yards per carry, but there have been very few explosive runs.

Against a Bengals offense that’s scoring 31-plus every week, a run game that keeps Joe Burrow on the sideline could be one of the most important factors.

Part Two: The Blitz Question

Now let’s get into the game plan, starting with the first question Quinn was asked.

Washington likes to blitz, and the Bengals have been very good against the blitz. A reporter asked why Burrow and that offense are so effective in those situations.

Quinn’s first answer was simple: they’ve been good against everything right now.

Then he broke it down. When you send extra rushers, you’re trying to disrupt timing. The question is how quickly the offense can answer and get into the right play.

So Quinn said disguise will be big in this game: when to blitz, when not to blitz, and how to send it. He said you have to take your shots, but the key is the scheme and the playmaking ability. And he pointed to Burrow’s ability to quickly manipulate the situation as a big reason he’s so hard to defend.

What That Means in Practice

Think about what that means.

A blitz works when the quarterback doesn’t know where the pressure is coming from until it’s too late.

But if the offense reads the look before the snap and adjusts, the extra rusher becomes a liability, because the defense has fewer players in coverage.

That’s why Quinn keeps coming back to disguise. If the defense makes everything look the same until the snap, the quarterback has to guess. And against a quarterback who reads defenses as well as Burrow, forcing him to guess is the only way a blitz becomes a real weapon.

Why It Matters for Washington

This connects to what we’ve talked about with Daronte Jones’s defense. His system, built on the Brian Flores-style disguise philosophy, is supposed to do exactly this: show one look and play another.

Early in the season, that system struggled. But over the last month, it’s started to work. And Monday night against Burrow will be the toughest test yet.

Part Three: The 2024 Game and Jayden’s Belief

Next, a reporter brought up the 2024 game in Cincinnati, during Jayden’s rookie season.

Quinn joked that he remembered Burrow completing a lot of passes early in that game, which got a laugh. Then he got to the point.

Looking back, Quinn said that game was probably the first big moment of belief for Jayden: the moment he learned to take his shot and be aggressive.

Quinn walked through the context. Washington had lost to Tampa Bay in Week 1 and hadn’t played well. Then it won its second game on field goals. And then came Cincinnati, and the big play to Terry McLaurin.

Quinn said that play displayed Jayden’s accuracy. Everyone in the building had seen it in practice, but seeing a big shot delivered to the right spot in a real game helped solidify his clutch ability. Quinn said it was nice for the team to experience that.

Can One Moment Change a Quarterback?

A reporter asked whether a moment like that can change a quarterback mentally.

Quinn said he thinks it can. Jayden was already a confident player, but anytime a quarterback delivers, it adds confidence to everyone around him.

It’s not just the performance, Quinn said. It’s the other players believing in him in that spot. They already believed before, but when you actually do it, it adds value.

Why This Matters Now

That answer is important, because Washington needs exactly that version of Jayden on Monday night.

Remember the stretch before the Giants game: 70 percent of his throws against the Rams were behind the line of scrimmage or within nine yards, and his last three games were among the most passive of his career.

Then, against the Giants, he took his shots and led the game-winning drive.

The Jayden who took the shot to McLaurin in Cincinnati in 2024 is the Jayden Washington needs again in 2026.

Part Four: The Receiver Room

Next, a reporter asked about the receiver group with Terry McLaurin out.

Quinn mentioned that one of his receivers has been battling through a foot injury, and that having him closer to full speed is good.

He said it took some time to get everyone in the right spots, but since the game in San Francisco, he thinks the group has felt more gelled. He likes seeing players featured in their roles.

His hope is to see more of that in the second half of the season. Now that the team has settled in, this is the group, and he likes its depth.

He also mentioned that a few weeks ago, more players were injured, and he credited his assistant coaches for doing a really good job getting backups ready.

Trey Amos: Back to Form

Quinn was also asked about cornerback Trey Amos, now that Amos is healthy.

Quinn said it took Amos a game or two to get back into it. It wasn’t just conditioning, but also his movement, his positioning, and finding the ball. Quinn thinks all of that has come together for him.

He said he’s excited, that things feel more settled, and that time and reps together are definitely part of the team’s improvement.

Remember, Amos returned in Week 6, and the pass defense has improved noticeably since then. He’ll be a key player against Ja’Marr Chase and Tee Higgins.

Part Five: Antonio Williams. More Touches Are Coming.

This might be the most important answer of the whole press conference.

A reporter noted that Antonio Williams is making the most of his opportunities. He had just two targets against the Giants but still found the end zone. The reporter asked whether Quinn wants to get the ball in Williams’s hands more.

Quinn said yes, and not only on third downs, but on early downs too.

He said one of the nice things about the bye week was that it came right in the middle of the season. It gave the staff time to ask what unique things Williams brings and how to feature him compared with other players.

Quinn said that’s what he’s looking forward to seeing in the second half, and that he loves where Williams’s development is heading.

Why This Is Big

We’ve been saying this on Commanders Floor for weeks. Against the Giants, Williams played 86 percent of the offensive snaps but got only two targets.

A third-round rookie who’s on the field almost every snap, and who keeps producing when he gets chances, needs more than two targets per game.

Now the head coach is saying it publicly: more touches, on first and second down too. That’s a big deal.

On Monday night, watch the target count. If Williams gets seven or eight targets, Quinn’s words are backed by action. If he gets two again, we’ll talk about it.

Part Six: Handling Momentum

Next came a big-picture question.

Quinn knows this market. You win a game and everything’s great. You lose a game and the sky is falling.

A reporter asked how much he thinks last Thursday’s win puts the team in a completely different place going forward.

Quinn’s answer was thoughtful. He said that in a competitive spot, when you take advantage of an opportunity, it provides proof that this is how you can operate. When you miss those opportunities, there’s regret.

So the proof, he said, is whether you can do it again, and then do it again. That’s the response he’s looking for from his team.

In other words, one win doesn’t change everything. Two wins in a row would start to.

Part Seven: Defending Chase and Higgins

Now let’s talk about Cincinnati’s weapons.

A reporter noted that the Bengals aren’t afraid to go to Ja’Marr Chase or Tee Higgins, and asked how Quinn decides which receiver gets more attention.

Quinn called it a difficult challenge. The running back gets targets. The tight end gets targets in the red zone. So everybody has a role, and you can’t double-team everybody. You have to pick your spots. Quinn said his team will have its ways, but having multiple players performing at a high level is one of the biggest challenges.

What Makes Each Receiver Unique

Another reporter asked what makes Chase and Higgins unique individually.

Quinn said they’re different in a lot of ways.

On Ja’Marr Chase, he said he respects Chase’s ability after the catch, his downfield throws, and his body control to go get the ball. But especially, his ability to break tackles in the open field and make people miss.

On Tee Higgins, Quinn said he always notices the downfield throws, his length, his extension, and his catch radius. You think a throw is going out of bounds, and then Higgins catches it.

So it’s not that one only works low and the other only works high. The difference is in how the Bengals use them after the catch. Both are excellent players with great catch radius, and both are great competitors.

Quinn’s summary: his team will have its work cut out for it, because it’s a very good tandem.

Part Eight: Complementary Football

Then came the question about the number itself: the Bengals have scored at least 31 points in each of their last five games. What kind of pressure does that put on Washington’s offense?

Quinn said the team talked a lot about how it wins. On what they call their Wednesday, they put together a big tape of how they win, and it wasn’t just one side of the ball.

He wants everyone to know this is how they’re going to prepare to win. He said they need a complementary game, which doesn’t mean one side has to carry everything. Everyone does it together.

Why That Answer Matters

That’s an important point.

A high-scoring opponent tempts a team to think its offense has to match every score. Quinn is rejecting that idea.

The defense and special teams have jobs too. And a complete performance is how you beat a team that scores at will.

We’ve heard “complementary football” from Quinn a lot this year, and sometimes it’s felt like a cliché. But against this Bengals team, it’s literally the only path. Washington probably can’t win a 45–42 shootout. It can win a 27–24 game with takeaways, a run game, and a couple of shots downfield.

Part Nine: The Bengals’ Path and Jayden’s Resilience

A reporter noted that the Bengals had a similar path to Washington: a quick start, followed by a couple of years where things didn’t go their way. Is there a lesson about patience and sticking to the program?

Quinn said injuries, especially Burrow missing time, were a big part of the Bengals’ struggles. Burrow is an incredible player, Quinn said, but beyond him, there’s a lot of resiliency and toughness throughout that team. It feels like they’ve turned the corner again, which is why it’ll be a great challenge.

Jayden’s Scars

Then the reporter asked how Jayden shows those same qualities, coming back from injuries in recent years.

Quinn said that when you go through scars, you don’t really know what you’ve learned until the next season and the next opportunity.

So having Jayden playing now, over an extended stretch, is winning football. Quinn said Jayden has been excellent in ball placement and accuracy, with more games in the system, and in and out of the pocket. The more turns and reps he gets, the more that will keep growing.

Part Ten: The Quarterback Mentality

Back to the 2024 game, a reporter pointed out that neither team punted that night, and asked what Quinn learned about Jayden drive by drive, and what it takes mentally for a quarterback to keep going back out knowing he has to score.

Quinn said that’s part of playing the position: answering again and again.

Then he said something revealing about the Giants game. When Jayden threw his interceptions, he was angry. And Quinn thought that was good, because it showed Jayden didn’t want to be the reason the team came up short.

Quinn said that’s different from other situations. In a back-and-forth game on the sideline, you’re just focused on the next drive, the next moment, the next series. And Jayden has always shown that.

Some games go that way, and some don’t, Quinn said. But you have to be ready to match when it’s called for.

Why That’s Interesting

That’s a revealing answer. Jayden threw two interceptions against the Giants, his first of the season. And instead of shrinking, he got angry and led the game-winning drive.

That’s exactly the trait you want on Monday night, when the Bengals might score on every drive.

Part Eleven: The Defensive Turnaround

Now let’s talk about the defense, because this was one of the most encouraging parts of the press conference.

A reporter noted that in the first five games of the season, Washington allowed 24 or more points every time. In the last four, it hasn’t allowed 24 to a single opponent.

What’s the pivot?

Quinn said, first, the ability to get takeaways, which has been significant.

Second, he said defensive coordinator Daronte Jones did a nice job taking a layer or two out of the game plan when something wasn’t hitting.

Third, the tackling has been better.

And fourth, he likes how the players are communicating, which is a big piece.

Busts Versus Good Plays

Quinn made an important distinction.

Early in the season, big plays happened because of busts: someone wasn’t where he was supposed to be.

Now, when an opponent makes a big play, it’s usually because it simply made a good play against a defender who had the right technique and the right leverage. You can live with that.

What stood out to Quinn was seeing the defense answer in critical four-minute situations, when the opponent is trying to run out the clock or put the game away.

The Numbers Back It Up

Remember the numbers:

49ers: 23 points.
Eagles: 10 points.
Rams: 20 points.
Giants: 21 points.

And Washington has forced a turnover in every game since Week 3.

That’s a completely different defense from the one in September.

Percy Butler’s Role

Quinn was also asked about safety Percy Butler, and what’s different about him defensively this year, given that his special teams impact is well known.

Quinn said it was good to get him more work. You feel his speed at safety, and Quinn said Butler and his safety partner are faster than the average safety.

On some third downs, the defense has to match up safeties on receivers, and with those two, Quinn feels good about it.

On special teams, Quinn said Butler brings energy and can make big plays as a gunner to flip field position. Defensively, he likes Butler’s ball skills and play speed.

Part Twelve: Why Teams Are Blitzing Jayden More

One of the most interesting questions: Washington has been blitzed more since Jayden came back than it was earlier in the season. Why?

Quinn called it a good question. He said it feels like the league overall is blitzing more, though he admitted he has no numbers to support that beyond his own team’s games.

Sometimes, he said, it’s the opponent. Washington has played one opponent twice that blitzes heavily. That’s the Giants.

And for the Rams, blitzing is a big part of their package.

San Francisco didn’t blitz as much, but that wasn’t the 49ers’ defensive style.

So part of it, Quinn said, is the systems of the opponents.

The Real Question

Then Quinn got to the key point. For the offense, the question is whether you can capitalize when a defense is that aggressive.

In the last game, he thought the answers to pressure were better.

And he pointed to the game-winning play against the Giants. It was a zero-pressure look, meaning an all-out blitz with no deep safety. Jayden got the offense into the right check, and the receiver ran a choice route.

Quinn said those are the moments when a defense is going to be aggressive, and you have to deliver.

That’s a perfect summary of what Washington needs on Monday: when the blitz comes, be ready to punish it.

Part Thirteen: Covering Big Receivers

The final question was about how cornerbacks should handle big, physical receivers like Chase and Higgins.

Quinn said it’s a difficult challenge. Any corner would tell you he wants to be in front of the receiver as often as possible, so that the receiver’s release goes lateral.

That means not giving receivers a free release, where they can run full speed off the line of scrimmage.

That doesn’t mean pressing every snap, Quinn said, but being in front makes over-the-top throws harder.

The Scramble Drill Problem

Then Quinn pointed to what makes it especially hard: the second-chance plays with Burrow.

When the play breaks down and the offense goes off schedule, it’s no longer a designed route. It’s a spot where Burrow can throw the ball. Quinn said those plays are as hard to guard as any, because a route concept has turned into a scramble drill.

He said that’ll be a big piece of the game.

Leverage

Quinn explained that leverage helps a lot. If a cornerback knows a safety is helping on the inside, he can keep outside leverage and know where his help is.

But the best receivers can still win by getting in front of a defender and shaking him, and Chase and Higgins can both do it.

So the question is whether a defender can hold his leverage, even through a contested catch.

Final Word From Commanders Floor

When you step back, Quinn’s answers have a theme.

The Bengals have a quarterback who reads pressure and turns broken plays into big ones, plus two receivers who win in different ways.

Washington’s answer is complementary football, disguise on defense, and a quarterback who has grown into taking his shots.

Here are the key takeaways:

One: Lucas Patrick is cleared and practicing, giving Quinn real options on the offensive line.

Two: Bill Croskey-Merritt is not ruled out for Monday, but Quinn wants to see him through a full week of practice.

Three: Quinn wants more touches for Antonio Williams, including on early downs.

Four: The defense hasn’t allowed 24 points in four straight games, thanks to takeaways, tackling, and communication.

Five: Disguise will be the key against Burrow’s ability to beat the blitz.

Six: Jayden’s 2024 performance in Cincinnati was a turning point for him, and Washington needs that version of him again.

Monday night will show whether that plan holds up against a team that hasn’t scored fewer than 31 points in five weeks.
