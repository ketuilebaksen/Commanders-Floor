The Washington Commanders just added an SEC Defensive Player of the Year. Not a Pro Bowler. Not a superstar. But a guy with real production, real upside, and a story worth telling.

And while Ashburn was making that move, the trade market exploded. Quenton Nelson, the All-Pro guard we begged Adam Peters to go get, just got traded, and the price will make you sick. DeForest Buckner is gone too. And K’Lavon Chaisson’s name keeps popping up as a player who could be on his way out of town.

There’s a lot to cover before Sunday’s Eagles game. Welcome to Commanders Floor. Hail to the Commanders. Let’s go.

Part One: The Market Just Exploded

Let’s start with the biggest headline of the week, because it changes how we look at everything else.

On Tuesday, the Indianapolis Colts traded Quenton Nelson to the Seattle Seahawks. Indianapolis gets a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick.

A third and a fifth, fam. For an eight-time Pro Bowler and six-time All-Pro, the best guard on the trade market.

The same day, the Colts traded DeForest Buckner to the San Francisco 49ers. A week after firing general manager Chris Ballard, Indianapolis has started a full-blown fire sale.

And here’s the detail that hurt me the most. This season, according to PFF, Nelson has an 85.7 overall grade through seven weeks, second among all left guards. He’s not declining. He’s still elite.

The team that got him? The Seattle Seahawks, the defending Super Bowl champions. They had an underperforming line, and they just fixed it at a bargain price. Nelson goes to left guard, and 2025 first-round pick Grey Zabel slides over to the right side. Seattle now has one of the best interior lines in the league.

Part Two: The Price That Should Bother Every Commanders Fan

Do you remember what we said on this channel last week? I told you that if Indianapolis made Nelson available, I’d give up a second-round pick today, no questions asked. The roundtable column said a second. The A to Z Sports trade simulator said a second.

The actual price was a third and a fifth.

So Washington could have had the shield Jayden Daniels has been begging for, a guard who allowed one sack all of last season, for less than what everybody projected. And he went to Seattle.

Now, let me be fair, because this channel doesn’t sell smoke. No insider ever reported Washington talking to the Colts. We don’t know whether Adam Peters called. We don’t know whether he offered something. Maybe Indianapolis simply preferred Seattle’s offer. There were other teams interested too. Reports say Minnesota had shown interest.

But here’s what we do know. Jayden got sacked four times on Monday night in San Francisco. This offensive line has used nine different combinations. Brandon Coleman is learning left tackle in the middle of the season. And the best interior lineman on the market went to a contender for a third-round pick.

If Peters didn’t make a serious offer for Nelson at that price, that’s a missed opportunity. If he did and got outbid by a third and a fifth, I want to know what he offered. Either way, the Commanders now have to find their offensive-line answers somewhere else.

Tell me in the comments: should Peters have beaten a third and a fifth for Quenton Nelson? Yes or no?

Part Three: The Signing. Meet Kyle Kennard.

Now let’s get to Washington’s move, because this one is more interesting than it looks.

According to reports, the Commanders brought in edge rusher Kyle Kennard for a workout and liked what they saw enough to add him to the practice squad.

I know what you’re thinking: a practice-squad signing, why make a whole segment about it? Because this isn’t just a random body. Let me tell you who this kid is.

Who Kyle Kennard Is

Kennard spent four years at Georgia Tech before transferring to South Carolina for the 2024 season, and his one year in the SEC was special.

He won SEC Defensive Player of the Year.

Think about what that means. It’s not the Mountain West or the MAC, no disrespect to either. This is the SEC, the closest thing the NFL has to a farm system. If the NFL had triple-A baseball, the SEC would be it.

In that one season at South Carolina, Kennard had eleven and a half sacks and three forced fumbles.

And he didn’t come out of nowhere. In 2023 at Georgia Tech, he had 53 tackles, six sacks, two forced fumbles, and an interception. This is a player who has filled up the stat sheet before.

The Draft and the Fall

The Los Angeles Chargers took Kennard in the fourth round of the 2025 draft, 125th overall.

And here’s a Washington connection. Dan Quinn met with Kennard at the 2025 NFL Combine and spoke about the qualities he saw in him, and reports said the Commanders had interest in him during that draft. So this isn’t a name the coaching staff just discovered on the waiver wire. Quinn has known about him for more than a year.

Then things went sideways. Kennard played sparingly as a rookie, appearing in only a handful of games. This preseason, he got hurt during a joint practice with the 49ers. The team didn’t have to say where the injury was, but Ben Standig later reported it was an ankle injury.

Between the injury and the limited rookie production, the Chargers made a surprising call and let him go with an injury settlement. Earlier this week, Washington brought him in for a workout, and now he’s in Ashburn.

Part Four: The Scouting Report

Here’s what the draft evaluators said about him coming out.

Lance Zierlein of NFL.com, one of the best draft evaluators out there, projected him as a fourth-round pick, which is exactly where he went. His evaluation, in my own words:

Kennard came out with a mountain of 2024 production. He keeps his long frame clean by landing well-timed punches, and he has good range as a playmaker. He can be slippery when he finds an edge, as a pass rusher and against the run. He creates pressure with his get-off and his length alone.

The concerns? He gets knocked off balance at contact too often. He needs less predictable rush patterns and better hand technique to beat NFL tackles. His best fit is as a wide-nine edge rusher, lining up outside where he can avoid early contact and build up speed into his rush.

Zierlein said the production looked like a Day 2 player, a second- or third-rounder, but the tape created enough concerns to lower his floor.

An anonymous NFC national scout was blunter: the production looks great, but there are too many snaps where he gets pushed around.

So the full picture is an up-and-down player who can produce, but who has bad moments too. He was SEC Defensive Player of the Year in 2024, barely saw the field in 2025, got hurt, and was released.

Part Five: Why I Like This Move

Let me tell you why this is a smart move, even if it’s not a flashy one.

One: it costs almost nothing. A practice-squad spot is the lowest-risk investment in football. If he doesn’t pan out, you’ve lost nothing. If he does, you found a contributor for free.

Two: the fit makes sense. Zierlein’s report says his best fit is as a wide edge rusher who can attack with speed and length. In Daronte Jones’s system, he’d be an outside linebacker coming off the edge.

Three: the timing. This move came as Washington’s edge group is right in the middle of trade-deadline conversations, and that brings us to K’Lavon Chaisson.

Four: Steve Wilks is in the building. Wilks has coached defenses all over this league. Giving a toolsy young pass rusher to a veteran staff for the rest of the season is exactly how you find out whether the 2024 version of Kyle Kennard is still in there.

He’s not a savior. He’s a lottery ticket. But it’s a smart lottery ticket, and Quinn has been interested in him since the Combine.

Part Six: K’Lavon Chaisson on His Way Out?

Now let’s talk about the bigger name.

Chaisson signed with Washington in March on a one-year, twelve-million-dollar deal, with 10.3 million guaranteed, right alongside Odafe Oweh, who got four years and a hundred million.

Coming off a career year with the Patriots, Chaisson had ten and a half sacks counting the playoffs, and he played in the Super Bowl. Washington brought him in to help turn one of the league’s worst pass rushes around.

This year, he has two and a half sacks. But he forced fumbles in back-to-back games, against the Giants and the 49ers, eight days apart.

And now his name keeps coming up in trade conversations. We already know The Athletic’s Daniel Popper put him on his deadline big board of players who could move. And with contenders desperate for pass rush, one-year players like Chaisson are exactly what buyers shop for.

Let me be clear, though: as of right now, no trade has been reported. His name is in the conversation, not in a deal.

My Stance Hasn’t Changed

You already know where I stand, because I said it on this channel: Chaisson is first in my selling order.

It’s a one-year deal, and in March he either walks or you hand a long contract to a guy with two and a half sacks this year. His value will never be higher than it is right now, after two straight forced fumbles. If someone offers a fourth, you take it. If someone offers a third, you take it and run.

And look at what the Nelson trade just told us. The market is paying third-round picks for elite players on expiring deals. A third for Nelson means sellers won’t get Day 2 hauls for good-not-great players. So if a contender offers a fourth or better for Chaisson, that’s a good deal in this market. Don’t wait for an offer that isn’t coming.

How Kennard Fits Into This

That’s why the Kennard move makes so much sense together with the Chaisson conversation.

If Chaisson goes, Washington’s edge rotation is Odafe Oweh, Dorance Armstrong, the other edge rushers already on the roster, and now a young SEC Defensive Player of the Year sitting on the practice squad, ready to be elevated if the staff likes what it sees.

You’re not replacing Chaisson’s experience with Kennard. But you’re giving yourself a young option to develop for the rest of the season, at no cost, while you turn an expiring contract into a draft pick. Sell what walks in March, and find out what you have in what’s cheap and young. That’s exactly the plan we’ve been pushing all week.

Part Seven: What the Nelson Trade Tells Us About the Deadline

Let’s zoom out, because the Nelson deal is a signal for the whole market.

For sellers, it says expiring contracts don’t bring back huge hauls. If an eight-time Pro Bowler on an expiring deal returns a third and a fifth, nobody should expect a second-round pick for Daron Payne in his final year. That makes the Payne-for-Brian Thomas Jr. idea even more attractive. Instead of hoping for a pick, you turn Payne into a 24-year-old receiver under contract for years.

For buyers, it says prices are lower than people projected. The benchmark for young receivers, according to Jaguars On SI, is the Marvin Harrison Jr. deal: a second and a swap of Day 3 picks. Washington needs to move before contenders like the Seahawks, Chiefs, and Steelers grab the best pieces.

For Washington, it says the clock is real. The deadline is November 10th, there are fewer than two weeks left, and the Colts’ fire sale started before anyone in Ashburn made a visible move. Contenders are acting. Peters can’t be late again.

Part Eight: Eagles Week

Before any of this matters, there’s a game on Sunday.

The Eagles come to Landover on November 1st. Jalen Hurts is on injured reserve and likely out for the season. Tanner McKee is at quarterback. Philly’s injury report has been brutal, and their offense has scored only six points in six third quarters this season.

This is a game Washington has to win. A win puts us at two-four-and-one, still alive in a division the Giants lead at four-two-and-one. A loss puts us at one-five-and-one, and then every conversation becomes about selling.

And yes, this is the first chance to see the tweaks Mike Garafolo reported were coming during the bye. It’s also the first game plan Steve Wilks has had two full weeks to work on with Daronte Jones’s staff.

If the edge rushers can make McKee uncomfortable early, especially after he lost eleven yards on the very first play of his first start, Washington can take control of this game.

Final Word From Commanders Floor

Let me put it all together.

Quenton Nelson is gone to the defending champion Seahawks for a third and a fifth. We said we’d pay a second. Washington’s offensive line still needs answers, and that missed opportunity should sting.

Kyle Kennard is in. He’s a former SEC Defensive Player of the Year with real college production, a fourth-round pick who got hurt and released by the Chargers, and a player Dan Quinn has liked since the 2025 Combine. It’s a low-risk, potentially high-upside move.

K’Lavon Chaisson is a name to watch as the deadline approaches. No trade has been reported, but he’s first in my selling order, and in this market, a fourth or better is a win.

And the Eagles come to Landover on Sunday, as beatable as they’ve been in years.
