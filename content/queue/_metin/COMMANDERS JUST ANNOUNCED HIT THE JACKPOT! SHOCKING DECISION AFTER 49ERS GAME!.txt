Sixteen days to the trade deadline, and Washington needs a weapon. Badly.

Terry McLaurin is done for the season. Jayden Daniels needs somebody who scares a defense. And two names are lighting up every Commanders group chat in America. One is the fastest man in football history, Tyreek Hill, still sitting on the open market. The other is a 24-year-old Pro Bowler who already caught seventeen touchdowns from Jayden Daniels in college, and who Ian Rapoport and Adam Schefter say is about to get a lot of phone calls.

So which one should Adam Peters go get? I’ll tell you, and I’ll tell you exactly what I’d pay. But to buy, you have to sell. The Athletic just put a price on four of our guys, and the first one I’m moving is going to hurt.

Welcome to Commanders Floor. Hail to the Commanders. Let’s go.

Part One: The Cheetah Dream

Let’s start with the name everybody wants to hear.

Tyreek Hill is an eight-time Pro Bowler. He has 819 catches, more than 11,300 yards, and 83 touchdowns in 145 career games. In 2023, he led the entire league with 1,799 receiving yards and thirteen touchdowns. He has a Super Bowl ring from Kansas City. Put simply, he’s a future Hall of Famer.

And he’s still available.

Picture it with me. Landover, a cold November night. Jayden Daniels drops back, and on the outside, wearing burgundy and gold, is the fastest player in the building. Before the snap, the safety is already backing up. Diggs gets one-on-one in the middle. The run game sees lighter boxes. And then, once a game, Jayden escapes the pocket, buys a second, and lets it fly. Hill runs under it, and it’s gone.

That’s the dream, fam. And this week, Hill posted another workout video. In July he admitted he had no power in his left leg, but the newer clips look a lot more explosive.

So yes, the dream is real.

Part Two: The Cheetah Reality

Now here’s the part you came to Commanders Floor for: the truth.

Hill still hasn’t signed anywhere. On October 5th, his agent Drew Rosenhaus said Hill could sign with a team within days. Three weeks later, nothing.

Why? The knee, for one. He dislocated it and tore multiple ligaments, including the ACL, in September 2025. He’s 32, and his whole game is built on speed. And according to The Athletic’s Jonathan Jones, Hill doesn’t want a veteran-minimum deal.

The biggest factor is the legal situation. Earlier this month, a civil jury in Miami found Hill liable on two counts of battery. Hill denies the abuse allegations, but the NFL is reviewing the case under its personal conduct policy, and that policy doesn’t require criminal charges for a suspension. Reports say that potential suspension is a big reason teams are hesitating.

Even the Chiefs, the team that knows him best, appear to be backing away. Andy Reid recently said nothing is happening on the Hill front, and the momentum behind that reunion has largely faded.

And one more thing: Rosenhaus has said Hill wants to play only for a contender. Be honest with yourself. Does a one-four-and-one team that just lost its best receiver look like a contender to Hill’s camp?

So here’s my stance on Hill. I’d keep the phone line open. If the NFL makes its decision, the doctors clear him, and he takes a short, incentive-based deal, I’d love to see what he has left. But I’m not building a trade-deadline plan around a player who might be suspended.

Because there’s a better weapon on the table. Younger, healthier, cheaper, and already connected to Jayden.

Part Three: The Other Name Moving Fast

Let me walk you through how this one moved over the last 48 hours, because it moved fast.

On Saturday, Jeremy Fowler of ESPN reported that several NFL executives believe Jacksonville will seriously consider trading Brian Thomas Jr. before the deadline.

Then Pro Football Rumors pulled it all together: Adam Schefter and Ian Rapoport expect calls to Jacksonville about BTJ to pick up over the coming days.

And Connor Hughes of SNY dropped something that blew my mind. Back in the offseason, the Jaguars were already prepared to trade him. They didn’t, because the offers weren’t good enough.

Think about that. Jacksonville was already trying to sell him in the spring. Today, with the player being used even less, his price is probably lower than it was then.

On Tuesday, Dan Graziano of ESPN said Thomas just might not fit in Jacksonville, and that a lot of people around the league believe Thomas could have a new home by the deadline.

Then The Sporting News, working off Graziano’s analysis, put together a list of landing spots that make sense: San Francisco, Las Vegas, the Giants, and Washington.

And today, ClutchPoints put out a price: a 2027 third-round pick and a conditional 2028 fifth. No first-round pick.

All the pieces are on the table, fam. A seller who already wanted to sell. Insiders saying the phone is about to ring. A buyer who just lost his number-one receiver. And a quarterback who already knows what it’s like to throw to this guy.

Part Four: Who BTJ Is

If you’re just getting here, here’s who we’re talking about.

Brian Thomas Jr. was the 23rd pick in the 2024 draft. He’s six-foot-three and 24 years old.

At LSU in 2023, the year Jayden won the Heisman, BTJ had 68 catches, 1,177 yards, and seventeen touchdowns. He led the entire FBS in receiving touchdowns, catching passes from Jayden Daniels.

As a rookie in Jacksonville, he had 87 catches, 1,282 yards, and ten touchdowns. That’s a franchise rookie record, and it came with a Pro Bowl.

For comparison, Terry McLaurin had 919 yards as a rookie.

Then came the drop. In 2025, Liam Coen arrived as Jacksonville’s head coach with a new staff, and BTJ fell to 48 catches, 707 yards, and two touchdowns.

This year, Jacksonville built its passing game around Parker Washington and Jakobi Meyers. BTJ is playing 43 percent of the snaps. He has nine catches for 105 yards and one touchdown in six games. In the last two games, he has zero catches on five targets, and against Houston he played fifteen snaps and didn’t come back after halftime.

Part Five: Why the Drop Doesn’t Scare Me

This is where I put my chips in.

A lot of people look at those numbers and say the kid fell off. I look at the dates and see something else.

BTJ was a Pro Bowler under one staff. A new staff came in with a new system, and he went dark. That’s not a player who lost his talent. That’s a player who doesn’t fit what a coach wants. Graziano basically said it: he might not fit in Jacksonville.

So the picture is clearer now. It isn’t a mystery why they’ve buried him. It’s a divorce with the system.

Now think about Washington’s system. What does Jayden need right now? A vertical threat on the outside, the role Terry used to play. A guy who forces the safety to stay deep and opens up the middle for Antonio Williams and the tight ends.

At LSU, BTJ was exactly that. Go routes, posts, the back-shoulder throw when the corner is sitting on his hip. Those seventeen touchdowns didn’t come on five-yard passes. They came with Jayden letting it rip and BTJ beating one-on-one coverage.

And you can’t buy chemistry. Back-shoulder timing, where the quarterback lets the ball go before the receiver turns because he knows exactly where he’ll be, gets built over hundreds of reps. These two already have them. Any other receiver you bring in at the deadline needs weeks to sync up with Jayden. BTJ needs one practice.

Part Six: The Contract, and the Best Part

Now the money, because this is where BTJ goes from nice to mandatory.

According to Spotrac, BTJ is on a four-year rookie deal worth 14.66 million dollars total, all of it guaranteed. This year, he counts just under four million against the cap. Next year, about 4.67 million. And there’s a fifth-year option for 2028.

Here’s my math, and heads up, it’s mine. If you bring him in at the deadline, you don’t pay that full four million. Jacksonville keeps carrying his signing bonus, and you only pay what’s left of his salary this season. That’s a fraction.

For comparison, Stefon Diggs counts eight million and walks in March.

So I’m not talking about a nine-game rental. I’m talking about Jayden’s number-one receiver for the next three seasons, for the price of a backup.

Compare that to Hill: 32 years old, coming off a reconstructed knee, wanting more than the minimum, and facing a possible suspension. Is it even close?

Part Seven: How You Pay for It

Here’s the full move, the one I’d make if I were Adam Peters.

On October 19th, the Rams lost to Arizona, and Davante Adams suffered a hamstring injury. Sean McVay said he’s out six to ten weeks.

On Thursday, Christopher Kline of FanSided pitched the Rams going after Stefon Diggs for a 2028 third-round pick. His argument was that, in terms of guaranteed production, the Rams won’t find much better than Diggs.

You see where I’m going with this. Diggs goes out: 32 years old and gone in March, for a third. BTJ comes in: 24 years old and under contract through 2027, with an option for 2028, for a third and a fifth. You swap an old rental for a young receiver, basically pick for pick, and you give Jayden his guy from LSU.

There’s one more twist. On November 8th, we play the Rams, and the deadline is the 10th. If Diggs goes before that game, he could be lining up against us. Picture it: Diggs in a Rams jersey on one sideline, BTJ in ours on the other. That kind of story could only happen in Washington.

Part Eight: The Arguments Against

I’ll give you the arguments against, because they’re out there.

One: zero catches in his last two games.

Two: he didn’t come back after halftime against Houston, and nobody has really explained why.

Three: Washington is one-four-and-one, and a team like that is supposed to sell, not buy.

Four: Diggs has four touchdowns, more than anybody else on the roster, and moving him leaves Jayden with less veteran experience in the red zone.

Here are my answers.

On the halftime thing: Washington’s doctors check him out before any trade gets signed. If he’s fine, end of discussion.

On selling versus buying: BTJ isn’t a buy for this year. He’s a buy for 2027. Selling what leaves in March and buying what stays is exactly what a team rebuilding the right way should do.

On Diggs: four touchdowns are worth less than three years of a 24-year-old receiver.

Part Nine: My Take on BTJ

Peters has to go get him this week. I’d pay the ClutchPoints price, a third and a fifth, without thinking twice.

And I’ll go further, even though I know it’s going to cost me in the comments. If Jacksonville asks for a second, I’d pay that too. For a 24-year-old Pro Bowl receiver under team control through 2028, who already has chemistry with your franchise quarterback, a second is cheap.

Will Peters be in that line? He said it himself on the radio: his phone is ringing. Washington needs a receiver more than almost any other team on Graziano’s list, and Jacksonville needs to move a player it doesn’t want to use anymore. When a seller who already wanted to sell and a desperate buyer look each other in the eye, the trade usually happens.

I think this can happen. And if it does, it’ll be Peters’s best move since he brought in Jayden.

Look, I could be wrong. Kansas City and Pittsburgh are also out there as receiver buyers, and Fowler says the Chiefs could be a top contender for BTJ, especially now that their Tyreek Hill reunion has stalled. Think about that for a second: the Chiefs may be pivoting from Hill to BTJ. If they come in hard, they could beat us to it.

So that’s all I’m asking. Adam Peters, don’t show up late.

Tell me in the comments, and no “it depends.” BTJ to Washington for a second-round pick: yes or no? And if you had to pick one, Tyreek Hill or BTJ?

Part Ten: To Buy, You Have to Sell

The Athletic has already put a price on our guys.

Daniel Popper of The Athletic built his big board of players who could move before November 10th, and Athlon Sports summarized it on Saturday. According to that summary, Popper says it’s time for Peters to sell and salvage what he can.

All 32 general managers read that ranking, and four of our guys are on it. If a contender is shopping for a pass rusher or a corner, now it knows which door to knock on.

There’s more. According to Athlon, Fox Sports’ Greg Auman had Daron Payne third among everybody available in the league. Third.

Let’s go one by one.

Daron Payne, number 13 on Popper’s board. Here’s the number that matters: a team that grabs him on deadline day would take on about 8.46 million dollars in cap, not 28. His cap hit is around 28 million, but his base salary is 16.4 million, and the buyer only pays what’s left of the season.

But look at this year’s numbers. According to PFF, cited by Athlon, Payne has 17 pressures, 10 run stops, one sack, five quarterback hits, and a 55.6 pass-rush grade. That ranks 108th in the league. One hundred and eighth.

On Friday, I said right here that Payne doesn’t get moved for a conditional fourth. Today, I’m adjusting that, and I’ll say it to your face: with the season at one-four-and-one, if somebody offers you a Day 2 pick, he’s gone. For less than that, it depends on what happens against the Eagles. But he’s not first in line.

Amik Robertson, number 18. According to Athlon, a buyer would take on 3.43 million in cap this year. His contract is two years and 16 million, but he has no fully guaranteed money in 2027. Trade him, and you’re left with three million in dead money next year. Popper lists Buffalo, Detroit, Cincinnati, Jacksonville, and Pittsburgh as fits.

His numbers are good. PFF has him at a 69.8 overall grade, 19th out of 102 cornerbacks. He has eight pass breakups in 389 snaps, the most of any corner in the league, with 22 catches allowed and a 92.9 passer rating in his coverage.

K’Lavon Chaisson. He’s on the board too, although Athlon doesn’t say where, and here’s the story nobody’s telling.

Chaisson signed with Washington in March for one year and twelve million dollars, according to NFL Network. One year. Last season with New England, he had ten and a half sacks counting the playoffs. This year, he has two and a half. But he forced fumbles in back-to-back games, against the Giants and the Niners, eight days apart. According to Commanders On SI, those two forced fumbles turned into as many recoveries as Washington had in all of 2025.

Dorance Armstrong. He doesn’t have a published spot either. According to Over the Cap, he signed in March 2024 for three years and 33 million dollars, with a fifteen-million-dollar signing bonus. This year he counts 11.577 million against the cap. Trade him or cut him now, and Over the Cap shows nine million in dead money. And he has zero sacks in 2026.

Part Eleven: My Selling Order

Now, what you came here for: my order. I’m warning you, the first one is going to make you mad.

First: Chaisson, right now.

Yes, I know. He’s our most productive pass rusher, and he’s the guy forcing fumbles. That’s exactly why.

It’s a one-year deal. In March, either he walks anyway, or you have to hand a long contract to a guy with two and a half sacks this year. His value is never going to be higher than it is today, after two straight forced fumbles, with contenders desperate for pass rush.

A one-four-and-one team doesn’t rent one-year players. It sells them. If they give you a fourth, you take it. If they give you a third, you take it and run.

Who gets pressure after that? Well, look at who’s getting paid to do exactly that. Armstrong is making 11.5 million dollars. If he can’t do the job, let the rest of the season tell you before March.

And don’t tell me selling in October means giving up. We already did it, and it worked. At the 2023 deadline, this same franchise sent Montez Sweat to Chicago for a second-round pick and Chase Young to San Francisco for a third. Two edge rushers and two Day 2 picks in one week. Athlon brought that up on Saturday precisely to argue for doing the same thing with Payne.

The difference is that in 2023, you were selling two young edge rushers. Today, you’re selling a one-year rental and a 28-million-dollar defensive tackle in his final year. The prices will be lower, sure, but the logic is the same: whatever walks in March, you cash in during October.

One tactical point, because somebody is going to bring it up. Without Chaisson on third-and-long, defensive coordinator Daronte Jones loses his best edge rusher and has to blitz more. That’s true. But look at the context. According to HB Analytics, we’re already 24th on third-and-long with Chaisson on the field. The third-down pass rush already isn’t working with him out there. What you lose on the field is less than it looks, and what you gain in picks is real.

Second: Payne, for a Day 2 pick. For less than that, it depends on the Eagles game. That’s the change of stance I promised you, and I’m making it out in the open. With what contenders pay for defensive tackles, and with a pass-rush grade ranked 108th, I can’t keep saying Payne stays at any price.

Third: Armstrong, and here’s the uncomfortable truth: you won’t be able to sell him. Zero sacks, 11.5 million in cap, nine million in dead money. Nobody gives up a pick for that.

Adam Peters signed that contract in his first March in Washington. It’s the mistake nobody mentions, because Andrew Wylie takes all the heat and Robertson’s play keeps him out of the conversation.

And Robertson? With eight pass breakups, the most in the league, he’s one of the few things working on this defense. If a contender blows you away, you listen. Otherwise, he stays.

Part Twelve: Putting the Whole Plan Together

Here’s the full Commanders Floor deadline plan, in one breath.

Sell: Chaisson for a third or a fourth. Payne for a Day 2 pick. Diggs to the Rams for a third if the offer is there.

Buy: Brian Thomas Jr. for a third and a fifth, and up to a second if you have to.

Watch: Tyreek Hill, only after the NFL decides his case, and only on a short, incentive-based deal.

Accept: Armstrong’s contract is a sunk cost, so you live with it and learn from it.

You come out of the deadline younger, with more picks, with Jayden’s college connection back in his hands for three more years, and with cap space protected for 2027. That’s not giving up on the season. That’s building the next three of them.

Final Word From Commanders Floor

Let me close it out.

Tyreek Hill is still the dream. But he’s still unsigned, the league’s review is still hanging over him, and even the Chiefs have cooled off. Until that changes, Washington can’t build a plan around him.

Brian Thomas Jr. is the real opportunity. He’s a 24-year-old Pro Bowler on a cheap, guaranteed rookie deal through 2027 with an option for 2028, already proven with Jayden Daniels, and Jacksonville has wanted to move him since the spring. The calls are about to pick up, so Peters can’t be late.

And to buy, you sell: Chaisson first, Payne for a Day 2 pick, and admit the Armstrong mistake.
