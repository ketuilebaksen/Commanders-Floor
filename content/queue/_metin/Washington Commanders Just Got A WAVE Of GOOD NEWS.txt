Commanders fans, the good news keeps rolling in before Sunday Night Football.

Washington’s injury report, which once had 23 or 24 names on it, is down to around eight or nine. Stefon Diggs is good to go. Key bodies are coming back. And on the other side, the Philadelphia Eagles’ injury report looks like a hospital waiting room.

On top of that, the trade market just told us exactly what Daron Payne is worth, and it’s not what most people expected. A defensive tackle just got traded for two first-round picks, another one went for a fourth, and Payne sits right in the middle of that conversation.

Welcome to Commanders Floor. Hail to the Commanders. Let’s go.

Part One: Washington’s Injury Report Is Finally Shrinking

Let’s start with the best news of the week.

There was a stretch this season when Washington’s injury report had 23 or 24 players on it. Every week, a new name. Every Wednesday, another body in the training room. You remember it, and so do I.

Now we’re down to roughly eight or nine. That’s a massive change, and it’s the biggest reason this week feels different.

Here’s who’s on the right side of that report.

Stefon Diggs is playing. He had a rest day on Wednesday and didn’t practice, but he was back on Thursday and practiced fully. With Terry McLaurin out for the season, that’s a huge deal. Diggs is now the top receiver in this offense, and Jayden Daniels needs him on the field.

Treylon Burks is back. He was limited on Wednesday with a foot injury, the same injury that knocked him out of the final drive in San Francisco before the bye. He looks good, and he’s expected to play. Without Terry, I think Burks becomes a big piece of this offense. When he’s healthy, he’s a pretty good receiver.

Charles Omenihu is back after dealing with groin and ankle issues. That’s another body for the defensive line rotation.

Johnny Newton’s 21-day practice window is open. That matters a lot this week, for reasons I’ll get to in a minute.

Jayden Daniels is on the report with his left elbow, but he’s practicing fully, so no concern there.

Marcus Mariota is back as the backup quarterback. Let’s hope we never need him on Sunday, because if Washington has to turn to the backup, the season gets very hard. But it’s good to have him healthy, and Washington’s quarterback depth is in better shape too.

Colson Yankoff is on the report with a knee issue, but he practiced fully on Wednesday and Thursday. He’s good to go.

Part Two: The Two Names That Hurt

It’s not all good news, and I’m not going to pretend it is.

John Bates has a shoulder injury and didn’t practice on Wednesday or Thursday. He’s one of the best blocking tight ends in the league, so if he can’t go, the run game and pass protection lose an important piece. That might be the toughest injury of the week.

Javon Kinlaw has a knee injury and didn’t practice Wednesday or Thursday either. Dan Quinn basically said in his press conference that Kinlaw is a long shot for Sunday. Losing him in the middle of the defensive line hurts, especially against a team that will want to lean on the run with a backup quarterback.

That’s exactly why Johnny Newton’s practice window opening is so important. If Kinlaw can’t go, and with Payne’s name all over trade rumors, Newton’s return gives this defensive tackle room a body it badly needs.

So yes, a couple of key guys are out. But when you’ve gone from 24 names down to nine, you accept a few absences. That’s football. The point is that Washington is finally getting healthier.

Part Three: The Eagles’ Injury Report Is a Disaster

Now let’s look across the field, and this is where it gets wild.

Here’s who didn’t practice on Wednesday for Philadelphia:

Saquon Barkley, hamstring and back. Jihaad Campbell, hamstring and quad. Britain Covey, their return man, and he’s a good one, with a knee injury. Jonathan Jones, hamstring. DeVonta Smith, hamstring.

And here’s who was limited:

Michael Carter II, abdomen. Jalyx Hunt, one of their young standouts, knee and ankle. Nolan Smith Jr., limited as well. Plus a few others.

There are more names on that report too: Zack Baun with a shoulder injury, Landon Dickerson with a pectoral injury, Dallas Goedert with a knee injury, Drew Kendall with a knee injury, and Tyler Steen with a shoulder injury.

Read that list again. Their star running back, their top receiver, their tight end, their Pro Bowl left guard, their return man, key defenders. It’s honestly insane how banged up this team is.

And that’s on top of everything else we’ve talked about. Jalen Hurts is out for the season after a blood clot diagnosis. Lane Johnson is retired. Cam Jurgens was traded to Baltimore. Tanner McKee is the quarterback. They just gave up 38 points to Dallas on Monday night and came out of it at two-and-five, with a short week to prepare and a road trip to Landover.

For once, Eagles fans are going through what Commanders fans usually go through. Some of them are already calling for their coach to be fired.

Part Four: No Excuses

Here’s the bottom line on Sunday’s game.

Washington is coming off a bye. Washington is getting players back. Washington is at home on Sunday Night Football, favored by four and a half points. Philadelphia is on a short week, coming off a blowout loss, with a backup quarterback and half its starters on the injury report.

Everything is shaping up the way the Commanders would want it.

So if you’re Dan Quinn and the Washington Commanders, you have to go take this game. There are no excuses if you lose this one. None.

Part Five: The Trade Market Just Spoke

Now let’s talk about the other big story this week, because the trade market moved fast, and it tells us a lot about Daron Payne.

Trade number one: on Tuesday, the Colts sent DeForest Buckner back to the San Francisco 49ers, the team that drafted him, for a 2027 fourth-round pick. Buckner is 32, a three-time Pro Bowler, in the final year of his deal, and has four sacks this season.

Trade number two: the same day, the Colts traded Quenton Nelson to Seattle for a 2027 third and a 2028 fifth. We already talked about how much that one stung.

Trade number three, the blockbuster: on Thursday, the Titans traded Jeffery Simmons to the Chicago Bears. Tennessee gets a 2027 first-round pick, a 2028 first-round pick, and a 2027 fourth-round pick. Chicago gets Simmons and a 2028 fifth-round pick.

Two first-round picks for a defensive tackle. According to ESPN’s Adam Schefter, that’s the first time an interior defensive lineman has been traded for multiple first-rounders since 1967.

Here’s the wildest part. The day before the trade, Simmons told reporters that as far as he knew, he was going to stay in Tennessee. Less than 24 hours later, he was a Bear, and he’ll reunite with his old college teammate, Montez Sweat.

Part Six: What These Trades Tell Us About Payne

So what’s Daron Payne actually worth? These trades give us the clearest picture yet.

First, let’s separate the two types of deals, because they’re completely different.

Simmons is 29, an All-Pro last season, and signed through 2030 after a three-year, 105.8-million-dollar extension this summer. Chicago isn’t renting him. It’s buying years of an elite player. That’s why it paid two firsts.

Buckner is 32, on an expiring contract, and heading to free agency. San Francisco is renting him for a playoff run. That’s why it cost only a fourth.

Daron Payne is the Buckner type of trade, not the Simmons type.

Like Buckner, he’s in the final year of his deal, his contract voids in February, and he’s a free agent in March. Any buyer knows it’s getting him for the rest of this season and nothing more, unless it can extend him.

Payne is younger than Buckner, 29 versus 32. But his 2026 production has been weaker. Remember the PFF numbers we covered: one sack, 17 pressures, and a pass-rush grade that ranks 108th in the league. Buckner has four sacks this year.

So if Buckner brought back a fourth, it’s hard to argue Payne brings back much more. I’d put his realistic value somewhere between a late third-round pick and an early fourth.

Why the Market Might Still Help Washington

Here’s the good news in all this: demand for defensive tackles is unusually high right now.

Look at what just happened. Within a few days, Buckner, Simmons, and Nelson were all moved. Contenders are clearly looking for interior help. Reports have also linked the Giants to Payne. And remember what we covered last week: Jacksonville is in desperate need of a defensive tackle.

When several contenders want the same position at once, prices can go up. That’s why I think Payne could still bring back a late third if Washington plays it right.

And this is the highest his value is going to get. Payne is 29. His career-best season was 2022, with eleven and a half sacks and a Pro Bowl. Honestly, I don’t think he reaches that number again. Whatever Washington can get for him now is probably the most it will ever get.

Part Seven: My Updated Stance on Payne

I’ve been honest with you about changing my mind as the market changes, so here’s where I stand today.

Payne has to go before November 10th. The worst thing Washington can do at this deadline is nothing.

If someone offers a late third, you take it. If the best offer is an early fourth, I still think you take it, because the Buckner trade just showed you that’s the market for an expiring defensive tackle.

But the best version of a Payne trade is still the one we talked about all week: Payne for Brian Thomas Jr. If the market for Payne alone is a fourth, a straight swap for a 24-year-old receiver under team control through 2028, with chemistry with Jayden, becomes an even better deal for Washington. You turn an asset worth a fourth into a potential number-one receiver.

And those draft picks matter more than usual. Teams around the league have been hesitant to give up 2027 picks because that draft class looks strong. If Washington can stack extra Day 2 and Day 3 picks in that draft, it helps this franchise no matter who’s coaching next year.

Part Eight: Sellers or Buyers?

Let’s be realistic about where this team is.

At one-four-and-one, Washington is a seller. That’s the honest answer.

But here’s how I’d frame it. Beat the Eagles, and you’re at two-four-and-one. Keep winning. Beat the Rams on November 8th, and you’re at three-four-and-one, right before the deadline. Then we can talk about buying.

Until those wins happen, the plan is selling what walks in March and buying only what stays for years.

Sell: Payne, the clearest trade candidate on the roster. K’Lavon Chaisson, on a one-year deal, has been in the rumor mill. Jeremy Fowler has mentioned Stefon Diggs as a possible trade name. And there’s Dorance Armstrong, if anyone will take that contract.

Buy: only young players with long-term control, like Brian Thomas Jr.

Part Nine: This Is Adam Peters’s Moment

Let’s talk about the general manager, because this deadline matters for him personally.

Adam Peters has drafted some players who haven’t developed as hoped yet. Critics point to Johnny Newton, a player many projected as a first-round talent who hasn’t broken out, and to other draft picks still looking for consistency. Brandon Coleman, forced into a tough spot at left tackle, has struggled this year.

Those critics might be too harsh on some of those players. Development takes time, and injuries have played a role. But what’s not up for debate is that this deadline is a big test for Peters.

In 2024, he had multiple second- and third-round picks and built a roster that reached the NFC Championship Game. If he can stack extra 2027 picks now, by selling Payne, Chaisson, and possibly Diggs, he gives this franchise real ammunition for the future, whoever the head coach ends up being.

If he does nothing, and lets Payne and Chaisson walk for nothing in March, that’s on him.

Part Ten: The Keys for Sunday

Before we close, let’s bring it back to the game, because the trade conversation depends on winning.

One: run at the Eagles’ defense. If Zack Baun and Jihaad Campbell are banged up or out, the middle of that defense is vulnerable. With John Bates possibly out, Washington will need others to step up in the run game.

Two: get Diggs and Burks involved early. With both healthy, Jayden has two veteran targets. Build rhythm early with Diggs underneath and Burks on the outside.

Three: pressure Tanner McKee. With Omenihu back and the defensive game plan built over two weeks with Steve Wilks in the room, Washington has to make life hard on a backup quarterback behind a banged-up line.

Four: stop the run without Kinlaw. If Kinlaw is out and Saquon plays through his hamstring, Payne and the rest of the interior have to hold up. Funny enough, this might be Payne’s final showcase in a Washington uniform, so expect him to play hard.

Five: no sloppy mistakes. The pre-snap penalties and second-down flags have killed this team all year. Against a team this beaten up, the only way Washington loses is by beating itself.
