Brian Thomas Jr. to Washington.

Sports Illustrated’s Jaguars site just ranked the Commanders as his number-one landing spot. ESPN says he’s the next big-name receiver getting moved before the deadline. And the idea taking over Commanders Twitter is simple: Daron Payne for BTJ, straight up, player for player. Jayden Daniels’s seventeen-touchdown weapon, back together.

But Jacksonville swears he’s not for sale. In a minute, I’ll tell you why I’m not buying it. Then we’ll talk about the broken Eagles team, with Tanner McKee at quarterback, that’s coming to Landover on Sunday.

Welcome to Commanders Floor. Hail to the Commanders. Let’s go.

Part One: Quick Context

If you’re just catching up, here’s the story.

Brian Thomas Jr. was the 23rd pick in the 2024 draft. At LSU in 2023, he was Jayden Daniels’s receiver during the Heisman season: 68 catches, 1,177 yards, and seventeen touchdowns. Seventeen, the most receiving touchdowns in all of college football.

As a rookie in Jacksonville, he had 87 catches, 1,282 yards, and ten touchdowns. That was a franchise rookie record, and he made the Pro Bowl.

From there, the curve went the wrong way. In 2025, he had 48 catches and 707 yards. This year, through six games, he has nine catches on twenty targets for 105 yards and one touchdown. He’s playing 43 percent of the snaps, with zero catches in his last two games.

Then came Wembley. In London against Houston, after a few drops in the first half, Liam Coen sat him for the entire second half, with Jacksonville trailing for most of the game.

Think about that. You’re losing, you need plays, and you leave your most physically gifted receiver on the bench. That tells you everything about where this relationship is.

Part Two: Washington, Number One on the List

Here’s what happened over the last 48 hours.

On Sunday morning, John Shipley of Jaguars On SI put out his ranking of the eight best landing spots for BTJ. Number one was Washington. After that came Las Vegas, the Rams, Atlanta, Pittsburgh, San Francisco, Seattle, and Kansas City.

His reasoning for Washington is exactly what we’ve been saying on this channel. Washington always made sense because of the relationship with Jayden. Washington badly needs receiver help right now. And, the line I love most, Washington needs a new future number-one receiver, and Thomas could be that guy. For Shipley, the move makes sense both short-term and long-term, even with Dan Quinn on the hot seat.

This morning, Heavy picked up that ranking and put it right in a headline: the best trade fit for BTJ is Washington.

The most important piece came from Shipley on Friday. In a separate article, he laid out three paths for Jacksonville with BTJ after the bye: trade him, sit him, or let him play his way out of the hole. He called a trade the most logical and most probable scenario.

And here’s the part that counts. If the Jaguars move him now, Shipley’s benchmark is what was paid for Marvin Harrison Jr.: a second-round pick plus a swap of Day 3 picks. Even if Jacksonville sits him, Shipley thinks it could still get a second.

Part Three: The Market Believes It Too

Now the part that blew my mind.

Sports Illustrated reported on Friday that on Kalshi, the prediction market, the odds of BTJ leaving Jacksonville before December 1st were sitting at 65 percent. At the start of the season, they were at nine percent.

A jump of 56 points. On that market, he’s the most likely player in the entire NFL to be traded.

The insiders are saying the same thing. Dan Graziano of ESPN wrote that Brian Thomas Jr. is going to be the next big-name receiver traded before the November 10th deadline. Jeremy Fowler, also of ESPN, said on SportsCenter on Saturday that several executives believe Jacksonville will seriously consider it.

And Fowler said one line that tells me everything: Jacksonville has relented in the past.

Fowler named Kansas City and Pittsburgh as receiver buyers. And in a Bleacher Report piece on the same topic, Washington shows up among the teams with a hole at receiver because of a major injury.

Part Four: The Other Side. Jacksonville Says No.

Now I’m going to give you the part you won’t like, because we don’t sell smoke on this channel.

On Sunday, Jaguars Wire wrote that Jacksonville has shown no signs of engaging in trade talks and doesn’t plan to trade Thomas. Heads up, though: it doesn’t say who reported that. It just says “reportedly.”

Tony Boselli, now a Jaguars executive and the biggest legend in that franchise’s history, said guys have bad days sometimes, that Brian Thomas is a Jaguar, and that the team knows he’s going to help them.

After Wembley, Trevor Lawrence said they obviously need to get him more involved, and that they’re going to need him.

So what do I do with that? Let me translate.

No team ever says publicly that it wants to sell a 24-year-old first-round pick. If Boselli goes on camera and says “we’re selling,” BTJ’s price hits the floor that same afternoon. That’s what teams always say.

What matters is what they do. What they did was sit him for an entire half in London. A team that has him in its plans doesn’t do that.

There’s one more thing working in our favor. The Jaguars were on their bye this week, and they come back Sunday against the Colts. If BTJ disappears again in that game, the conversation in Jacksonville changes on Monday, November 2nd. Washington has to have the phone in its hand that day.

Part Five: Why BTJ and Not Somebody Else

The door next to this one just closed.

On Sunday, Ian Rapoport and Adam Schefter said Cleveland isn’t shopping Jerry Jeudy. With the Browns at four-and-two, getting him out of there would take a premium price, and that’s a premium price for a 27-year-old receiver with twelve catches. No thanks.

So right now, the real market for a young receiver on a cheap contract with a Pro Bowl ceiling comes down to one name. And that name has seventeen touchdowns with our quarterback.

Part Six: The Tactical Side

This is what matters most to me.

Without Terry McLaurin, Washington lost its vertical threat on the outside. Stefon Diggs plays more in the slot and lives on short and intermediate routes. Antonio Williams, the rookie, has eighteen catches for 205 yards, 11.4 yards a catch. That’s really solid, but he also works the inside.

So what happens when nobody forces the safety to stay deep?

The safety comes down, and defenses play you with an extra man in the box. The corner presses at the line because he isn’t scared of the go route. Everything shrinks for Jayden.

BTJ is exactly the piece that gives this offense its spacing back. At LSU, those seventeen touchdowns came on go routes, posts, and back-shoulder throws. Jayden let the ball go before BTJ even turned around, because he knew exactly where he’d be.

You can’t buy that timing at the deadline. These two already have it.

Part Seven: The Money

According to Spotrac, BTJ is on a four-year rookie deal worth 14.66 million dollars, all of it guaranteed. He counts just under four million against the cap this year and about 4.67 million next year.

Then there’s the fifth-year option for 2028. Jacksonville has to decide on it in May, and with these numbers, nobody in Florida wants to pick it up.

For Washington, it’s the opposite. It’s a cheap, controllable receiver for Jayden’s entire window.

Part Eight: The Straight Swap. Payne for BTJ.

This is the part that gets me the most fired up today.

In that Heavy piece, David Harrison of Locked On Commanders said he thinks exploring the trade makes a lot of sense. And he floated the idea that has been going around other outlets too: Daron Payne for Brian Thomas Jr., player for player, no picks.

Think about that for a second.

Payne is 29. He’s in the final year of a four-year, ninety-million-dollar deal, and he’s a free agent in March. Washington didn’t extend him this offseason. He has fourteen tackles in six games. He’s a player who is leaving.

And what is Jacksonville right now? A team that was leading the AFC South before London, that’s built to compete, and that, according to Harrison, has already been linked to Payne in some outlets.

A contender trying to win this year needs a defensive tackle who eats double teams in January. It doesn’t need a receiver its head coach benches at halftime.

So Washington sells something that’s leaving in March and buys something that stays three years. Jacksonville sells something it isn’t using and buys something that helps right now. Both sides win.

It’s the most logical trade idea I’ve read all month.

Part Nine: The Buts

I could be wrong, so here are the buts.

One: the money on Payne’s side. Payne carries a lot of cap. A team that takes him on deadline day pays about 8.5 million dollars for the rest of the season. Jacksonville has to be willing to eat that.

Two: the medical question. Somebody needs to check why BTJ didn’t come back in the second half in London. The reports point to a performance decision, but if there’s anything physical behind it, the whole idea falls apart.

Three: the competition. Kansas City and Pittsburgh want receivers too. If the Chiefs offer a second-round pick, they beat us to it.

Four, and this is the most important one: no insider has said Washington made a call. This is an idea, not a negotiation. I want to be crystal clear about that.

Part Ten: My Take

Here’s what I’d do, and I have no doubts about it.

If I’m Adam Peters on Monday, November 2nd, after Jacksonville plays the Colts, I’m calling. I offer Payne for BTJ. If they ask for a Day 3 pick on top, I throw it in. If they want a second-round pick instead of Payne, I pay that too.

A 24-year-old receiver with chemistry with my quarterback, under team control through 2028 if I want him, is worth a second.

Isn’t that contradicting all the selling talk? No. That is selling the right way. What’s expiring goes out, and what comes in is young and cheap.

Peters said on the radio a week ago that his phone is ringing and they’re making calls. Well, this is the call.

Tell me in the comments, and no “it depends.” Payne for BTJ straight up: yes or no?

Part Eleven: First, You Have to Win Sunday

For any of this to make sense, you have to win on Sunday. And the team coming to Landover is in worse shape than you think.

Take notes, because this doesn’t happen often.

The Eagles are two-and-four with four straight losses. Jalen Hurts has been on injured reserve since October 17th, and NFL Network and ESPN say he’ll probably miss the rest of the season.

The quarterback is Tanner McKee. In his first start, against Carolina on the 18th, the Eagles lost. McKee went 26 of 45 for 215 yards and one touchdown, and took three sacks, including an eleven-yard loss on the very first play of the game.

The whole offense put up 222 net yards, 35 of them rushing, and only 45 yards on its first twenty plays.

And here’s a crazy one: the Eagles have scored six points in six third quarters this season, one of the worst marks in the NFL.

McKee said himself that it falls on the quarterback. Nick Sirianni said they’re going to have to do different things to help the offense get going.

The injury report heading into their game against Dallas looked like a horror movie. DeVonta Smith, out with a hamstring injury. Nolan Smith, out with a concussion. More names on the report, including cornerback Michael Carter II with an abdominal issue, and Saquon Barkley dealing with a hamstring.

Dallas was a four-and-a-half-point favorite in Philadelphia. Two years ago, that would have been unthinkable. Dallas favored at the Linc. Let that sink in.

Part Twelve: What Eagles–Cowboys Means for Us

Both the Eagles and Cowboys came into that game at two-and-four. The winner moves to three-and-four, and the loser drops to two-and-five.

Meanwhile, the Giants beat Houston in overtime on Sunday and sit on top of the NFC East at four-two-and-one.

If Dallas won, the Eagles arrive in Landover on a five-game losing streak with their heads somewhere else. If the Eagles won, they arrive with a little breathing room. Either way, given what’s in their training room, it doesn’t change much. This is a beatable team.

Part Thirteen: How You Beat This Team

Here’s the game plan I want to see on Sunday.

One: make McKee uncomfortable from the very first snap. Philly lost eleven yards on the first play of his first start. A young backup quarterback who gets hit early starts seeing ghosts. Daronte Jones and Steve Wilks have had two weeks to prepare. Show him looks he hasn’t seen on tape.

Two: stop the run, and make McKee beat you. Philly ran for just 35 yards against Carolina, and Saquon is banged up. If you take the run away, the whole game lands on McKee’s arm, and that’s exactly where you want it.

Three: own the third quarter. Six points in six third quarters. This team doesn’t come out of halftime well. Washington has given up second-half points all season, so this is the game where that has to flip. Come out of the locker room and put them away.

Four: give Jayden time. We talked all week about the offensive line, the second-down penalties, and the third-and-longs. Against a Philly defense missing pieces, Washington has to stay ahead of the chains. Clean second downs mean manageable third downs, and that’s where Jayden wins.

Five: show us the tweaks. Mike Garafolo reported changes were coming during the bye. Sunday is when we find out whether they’re real. If we see something different and visible, great. If it’s the same team with the same mistakes, the questions about this staff get even louder.

Why Sunday Changes Everything

Here’s why this game matters so much, beyond the standings.

A win puts Washington at two-four-and-one, with a real argument that the season isn’t over in a division where nobody is running away. It strengthens the case for buying a young piece like BTJ, because you’re adding a weapon to a team that’s still fighting.

A loss puts Washington at one-five-and-one. The pressure on the coaching staff goes through the roof, and the deadline conversation becomes all about selling.

Either way, the BTJ logic doesn’t change, because he isn’t a rental. He’s a three-year piece. But a win on Sunday makes Monday’s phone call a lot more fun.
