As the Commanders push toward a possible playoff run in the second half of the season, they have a massive decision to make on the offensive line.

And don’t look now, but Laremy Tunsil just took another step toward getting back on the field.

So today we’ve got a triple dose of great news. One: Nick Allegretti just played one of the best games of his career at center. Two: the snapping problem that’s been quietly throwing off Jayden Daniels’s rhythm might finally be solved. And three: Washington’s five-time Pro Bowl left tackle is moving forward in his recovery.

Welcome to Commanders Floor. Hail to the Commanders. Let’s go.

Part One: A Season-Long Offensive Line Saga

Washington’s offensive line has been a source of debate almost all year, going back to February, when Adam Peters and company decided to release center Tyler Biadasz.

That move surprised a lot of people. Biadasz didn’t have a great 2025 season and wasn’t close to his 2024 standard. But very few people expected Peters to just pull the plug.

Remember, Biadasz was one of the founding members of this rebuild, part of the first group of three-year deals Washington handed out, along with Frankie Luvu and Dorance Armstrong.

Washington let Biadasz go and went after Tyler Linderbaum. Linderbaum went elsewhere.

The Center Carousel

What followed was a carousel:

Nick Allegretti was named the starter.
Then he got hurt in training camp and barely practiced.
Julian Good-Jones started at center for most of training camp.
Matt Goldman got some work too.
Allegretti returned after about a week of practice and started against the Eagles in Week 1.
He started for three weeks, struggled, and was benched.
Lucas Patrick took over.
Patrick went into the concussion protocol against the Rams.
Goldman finished that game.
And then, on a short week, Allegretti got the call again for Thursday night in New York.

It’s Not Just Center

It’s not just the center position either.

Matt Goldman played right guard when Sam Cosmi was in the concussion protocol.

Brandon Coleman was supposed to play left guard this season. Then he moved to left tackle because Laremy Tunsil tore his triceps in training camp.

Now Washington heads into a crucial Monday Night Football game against the Bengals, and based on what we saw against the Giants, Allegretti might have earned the right to keep the center job.

If that’s the case, what happens to Lucas Patrick? And what if Tunsil can actually play again before the season’s over?

Let’s get into all of it.

Part Two: Good News Number One. Allegretti’s Big Night.

As I’m putting this together, Lucas Patrick is still in the concussion protocol. Nobody knows yet whether he’ll clear it in time for Monday night.

But let’s imagine he does. Let’s pretend he’s been practicing all week and is good to go. Even then, I think Allegretti has earned serious consideration to stay at center.

The Context Makes It Even More Impressive

Think about the circumstances of Thursday night.

It was a short week. Allegretti was changing positions. Yes, he’s said he’s been snapping the ball almost every day since he was about 14 years old. He’s been an emergency center on different teams, and he played some center in college. Nobody’s doubting his background.

But remember what Dan Quinn said after the Rams game, when he was asked why he put in Goldman instead of Allegretti after Patrick’s concussion: Allegretti had been practicing at guard, and the team didn’t want him making that adjustment on the fly.

So Allegretti had been practicing at guard. Then, on a short week, he got the call to play center.

Against One of the Most Aggressive Defenses in the League

And it wasn’t just any defense. It was the New York Giants.

Since Brian Burns tore his ACL in Week 3, only the Minnesota Vikings have blitzed more than the Giants. This is a very aggressive defense that brings stunts, twists, and extra rushers. The center has to communicate all of it and get it picked up on the fly.

And remember the first meeting with the Giants. In that game, the Giants came back because they completely stalled Washington’s offense. Washington had no answers for their pressure packages, especially the Cover 0 looks, where the defense blitzes with no deep safety.

How Allegretti Responded

On Thursday night, Allegretti played a very good game against that defense.

There’s a lot more to it, of course. Jayden did a nice job staying out of trouble and getting the ball out quickly. And I think this was David Blough’s best game as an offensive coordinator. The pass-protection plan was dramatically better than it was just four days earlier against the Rams.

But Allegretti was a big part of it. According to Pro Football Focus, he gave up just one pressure the entire game.

And ironically, that one pressure came on the game-winning touchdown from Jayden to Stefon Diggs. Allegretti got beaten badly on that play, but Jayden delivered the ball anyway, and Diggs did the rest.

One pressure allowed, in a short-week road game, against one of the league’s most blitz-heavy defenses. That’s an impressive night.

Part Three: Good News Number Two. The Snapping Problem.

This is something I’ve talked about only briefly on this channel, but other analysts have been all over it.

Lucas Patrick offered a noticeable improvement as a pure blocker. But his snaps have consistently been very high. We’re talking ten or more high snaps per game on passing plays alone.

And that doesn’t even count the running plays, where Jayden has to jump to grab the ball before he can hand it off.

Why It Matters So Much

Let’s talk about why that’s such a big deal, because it’s not as obvious as a missed block.

Jayden has been playing good, but not great, football for much of this season. He’d been protecting the football, which is arguably the most important thing a quarterback can do. But in doing so, he’d been turning down big plays and potential scoring opportunities.

There’s been bad play calling and injuries around him, and the team was losing games. It’s a complicated conversation, and we’ve covered it a lot on this channel.

But here’s the point: if you want a quarterback in rhythm, the worst thing you can do is constantly throw off his timing. And there’s a very easy way to do that: bad snaps.

What a Bad Snap Does

Think about it.

If the snap comes in high, Jayden has to jump up to grab it, like he’s Yao Ming. When he lands, his feet aren’t where he wants them to be.

Then the timing between when he reaches the top of his drop and when the receiver breaks on his route is thrown off.

And then the pass protection is affected, because the offensive tackles are protecting a spot in the pocket where they expect the quarterback to be. If he isn’t there, the pass rushers’ aiming point changes.

It has a huge impact on everything that happens on the play. It’s not as simple as pointing at a guard who whiffed on a block and gave up a sack. But it absolutely matters.

Allegretti Changes That

Allegretti looked improved as a blocker on Thursday, and he’s a much more consistent snapper than Patrick.

If nothing else, that opens up a conversation Washington probably never expected to have when it benched Allegretti after Week 3.

And think about the timing. Thursday night was the first game in weeks where Jayden looked truly comfortable in critical moments: the final drive, the deep shot to Treylon Burks, and the dart to Diggs. Is it a coincidence that it came in the game with clean snaps? Maybe. But it’s worth noticing.

Part Four: Being Fair to Allegretti’s Early Struggles

Let’s remember what Allegretti dealt with early in the season.

He barely practiced during training camp because of a calf injury. He basically got one week of practice before the season started. And he didn’t play well in the first three weeks.

I said on this channel at the time that I wasn’t really going to criticize him, because I didn’t expect him to play well under those circumstances. A center who misses camp, comes back a week before the opener, and immediately faces the Eagles is set up to struggle.

And we also know, from a recent interview, that Allegretti was struggling mentally during that stretch, and that he sought help from a counselor. He called it the best decision he’d ever made.

So to see him come back on a short week and play this well, against this defense, in this game, is a real comeback story.

Part Five: The Big Decision. Who Plays Where?

So here’s the decision Washington faces.

Option one: Allegretti stays at center, and Patrick returns there when healthy. That’s the “return to normal” option. Patrick is the better pure blocker, but the snap issues come back.

Option two: Allegretti stays at center, and Patrick moves to left guard. Patrick has had a solid year as a blocker. If he’s healthy, moving him to guard could give Washington the best of both worlds: Allegretti’s clean snaps and communication at center, and Patrick’s blocking strength at guard.

Option three: Allegretti stays at center, and Patrick becomes a backup. That would be the simplest choice, but it leaves a good blocker on the bench.

My Take

I like option two.

If Patrick clears the concussion protocol, I’d keep Allegretti at center and give Patrick a look at left guard.

Here’s why:

Allegretti just proved he can handle center against an elite blitzing defense.
His snaps are cleaner, and that directly helps Jayden’s rhythm and timing.
Patrick’s best trait is his blocking, which translates to guard.
It gets your five best offensive linemen on the field together.

But one caution: constantly shifting players is part of what’s made this line so unstable. Whatever Washington decides, it needs to commit and let the group build chemistry for the rest of the season.

Part Six: Good News Number Three. Laremy Tunsil’s Recovery.

Now for the biggest domino of all.

Remember the timeline. Tunsil tore his triceps during a training camp practice in early August and had surgery. At the time, Adam Schefter said he’d be surprised if Tunsil played at all this year. The AP reported, citing NFL Network, that he was expected to miss most of the season.

But Washington made a very smart move: it waited until after setting the initial 53-man roster to place Tunsil on injured reserve. That allowed the team to designate him to return at any point during the regular season or playoffs.

The Latest

Now there’s encouraging news.

According to CBS Sports, Tunsil is taking the next step in his recovery program. He’s still a ways from playing football, but the consistency of his recovery leaves the door open for an eventual return.

Nicki Jhabvala of The Athletic reported that Tunsil could potentially return late in the season.

According to Commanders.com, there’s still no timetable, but there’s hope he can return near the end of the season. Dan Quinn said the doctors were pleased with Tunsil’s last checkup, though he’s still in the healing process.

Why Tunsil Matters So Much

Let’s remember who Tunsil is when he’s healthy.

He’s a five-time Pro Bowler. In 2025, he allowed just two sacks in fourteen regular-season games. And Washington signed him to a two-year, 60.2-million-dollar extension.

Even while he’s been out, he hasn’t disappeared. According to Commanders.com, Tunsil has stayed at the facility and has been mentoring the younger linemen.

What a Tunsil Return Would Mean

Think about the domino effect if Tunsil comes back.

Tunsil returns to left tackle. Brandon Coleman, who’s had a rough year learning left tackle on the fly, can slide back inside.

Coleman moves to left guard, the position he was originally supposed to play this season.

And suddenly, Washington’s offensive line could look something like this: Tunsil at left tackle, Coleman at left guard, Allegretti at center, Sam Cosmi at right guard, and the right tackle in place.

That’s a completely different line from the one that couldn’t handle Myles Garrett against the Rams.

Let’s Be Realistic

I want to be careful here. A return near the end of the season is a hope, not a guarantee. Triceps tears are serious, and Tunsil will need to be cleared medically and ready to handle a full NFL workload.

But for a team that’s two games out of first place with the tiebreaker over the Giants, a healthy Tunsil in December or January could be a massive boost, maybe even the biggest “addition” any NFC East team makes this season.

Part Seven: What This Means for Jayden Daniels

Let’s tie this back to Jayden, because everything on the offensive line ultimately comes back to him.

Remember what we talked about earlier this month:

Against the Rams, 70 percent of Jayden’s throws were behind the line of scrimmage or within nine yards.
His last three games before the Giants were the third-most passive stretch of his career.
One reason: an offensive line that kept changing, and a quarterback who’d been conditioned by pressure to get rid of the ball early.

Then against the Giants, with a better protection plan, cleaner snaps, and Allegretti communicating well, Jayden threw three touchdown passes and led his first game-winning drive in almost two years.

That’s not a coincidence. A stable, communicative offensive line is the foundation for the aggressive Jayden this team needs.

Part Eight: The Bengals Test

Monday night against the Bengals is the next big test.

Here’s what to watch on the offensive line:

One: who’s at center? Does Washington stick with Allegretti?

Two: Lucas Patrick’s status. Does he clear the concussion protocol, and if so, where does he play?

Three: the snaps. Does Jayden get clean snaps all night?

Four: the protection plan. Does Blough’s plan look like the Giants game or the Rams game?

Five: the run game. With Jacory Croskey-Merritt expected back from injured reserve, can the line open lanes for a real ground game?

And of course, on the other side, Joe Burrow and Ja’Marr Chase will test Washington’s defense. But if this offensive line holds up, Washington has a real chance to win a second straight game and keep climbing the standings.
