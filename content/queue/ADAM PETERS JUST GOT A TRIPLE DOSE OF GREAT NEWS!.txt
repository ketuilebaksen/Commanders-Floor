Everybody called Adam Peters a passive general manager. And this week, everybody might have been wrong.

ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler just reported that the last lock is off, and Jayden Daniels is the one who opened it. There’s a receiver Washington could land without spending so much as a seventh-round pick. I’ll tell you who it is, and why Peters may have been sitting on this since July.

So yes, Peters just got a triple dose of great news. A broken relationship got fixed. The NFL’s waiver rules line up in Washington’s favor. And his franchise quarterback is leading the building into a primetime showdown with a three-word message.

Plus, Jonathan Allen comes back to Northwest Stadium on Monday in the wrong jersey, and I’m defending the call that hurt the most. And Joe Burrow is the MVP favorite, but Vegas might have forgotten what happened in 2024.

Welcome to Commanders Floor. Hail to the Commanders. Let’s go.

Act One: The Fight

To understand why this week matters so much, you have to go back to July.

This summer, Brandon Aiyuk posted things about Jayden Daniels online. And he didn’t stop at Jayden. He brought Jayden’s mom, Regina Jackson, into it.

I’m not going to repeat those posts. There’s no need. It’s enough to know what happened next.

On November 4th, Dan Graziano of ESPN said he didn’t expect Washington to go after Aiyuk, and he explained why: those posts.

And on this channel, we said it plainly: the key to Aiyuk was in Jayden’s pocket. No general manager in his right mind puts a receiver in that locker room who insulted the family of his franchise quarterback.

That was fair. I agreed with it. If your quarterback doesn’t want him, no contract in the world matters.

Act Two: The Forgiveness

Then, on Thursday afternoon, Jeremy Fowler reported on ESPN that Aiyuk and Jayden have patched up their relationship after their falling-out earlier this year.

They fixed it, and the details matter.

According to Fowler, Aiyuk was the one who reached out, about a month ago. And Jayden, again per Fowler, was receptive and understanding.

Look at the order of events. Aiyuk reached out in mid-October. On November 9th, the league reinstated him. And only now are we finding out.

So that conversation happened quietly, while all of us, me included, were saying the door was shut.

And here I have to keep it real: I was wrong. I thought Jayden would never forgive him. I’m glad I was wrong.

The Second Half of Fowler’s Report

Fowler’s report has a second half, and I’m not going to hide it from you.

He said he isn’t sure what this means for Aiyuk’s chances, or for Aiyuk’s previously stated desire to play in Washington. But he and the quarterback are good again.

Look at the phrase sitting in the middle of that report: his previously stated desire to play in Washington.

That desire is something Aiyuk had already put out there. I’m not making it up. ESPN reported it.

Act Three: The Play

Now let’s get to what almost nobody is explaining: the rule book. Because this is where the masterclass lives.

Aiyuk is still technically a 49er. The league reinstated him on November 9th from the list for players who leave their team.

Since that day, San Francisco has had a roster exemption. The 49ers don’t have to put him on the 53-man roster, and they don’t have to pay him.

That exemption runs out after Week 11. Most outlets say Monday, November 23rd. Pro Football Talk says the 24th. Either way, it’s this week.

The 49ers Are Stuck

Mike Florio of Pro Football Talk wrote that the 49ers have shown no inclination to release Aiyuk until they absolutely have to.

And Florio went further: if the 49ers are holding onto him just out of spite, he called it a “bush league” move that creates bad karma. Those are Florio’s words, not mine, but I’ll sign my name next to them.

49ers general manager John Lynch only said the situation is “fluid.” In general-manager language, that means they still don’t know how to get out of it.

So what are San Francisco’s options? Two: put him on the roster and pay him, or release him.

Kyle Shanahan already told you what he thinks. On November 9th, asked about Aiyuk’s reinstatement, he said it meant absolutely nothing to his team.

And Aiyuk’s agent, Drew Rosenhaus, told Dianna Russini that they didn’t even discuss him staying in San Francisco, because the writing has been on the wall for a while.

So the most likely outcome is that the 49ers release him.

The Detail That Flips Everything

Here’s the detail that changes everything.

Since we’re past the trade deadline, any player who gets released goes through waivers, even a veteran. And the waiver order goes from the worst record to the best.

That’s one of the few perks of being three-five-and-one. Washington would get to claim him ahead of every team with a winning record, ahead of all the contenders that might want him.

Why This Makes Peters Look Smart

Now think about what Peters did, and what he didn’t do.

In July, San Francisco wanted to trade Aiyuk. Peters didn’t pay.

At the deadline on November 10th, Peters didn’t pay either.

And the people criticizing him, including me at times, said he was a passive general manager who wouldn’t make a move.

But look where we are now. If the 49ers release Aiyuk, Washington could land him without giving up a single draft pick. Zero. Not even a seventh-rounder.

On top of that, according to the Associated Press, the league told Aiyuk he owed San Francisco around thirteen million dollars in bonuses he’d already been paid. That debt is between Aiyuk and the 49ers. It’s not Washington’s problem.

And one more detail that makes me smile: when San Francisco drafted Aiyuk in the first round in 2020, who was in that front office? Adam Peters. Peters knew this player before anybody in Washington knew his name.

That’s what a masterclass looks like. You wait. You don’t pay the desperation price. You let the other team run out of options. And you’re first in line when the player shakes loose.

Others See It Too

I’m not the only one seeing it this way. Heavy wrote this week that all signs point to Aiyuk signing with Washington.

That’s an outlet’s read, not a sourced report. But when even sites covering the 49ers are talking about Washington as the landing spot, something is moving.

Can It Go Wrong?

Of course it can.

Peters might not claim him. A team with a worse record could claim him first. San Francisco could decide to keep him out of stubbornness. And if nobody claims him, Aiyuk becomes a free agent and chooses for himself.

Remember, we just watched Hollywood Brown clear waivers without Washington putting in a claim. So nothing is guaranteed.

But if Aiyuk does become a free agent, where’s he going to want to go? Fowler already told you what his stated desire was.

Part Four: What Aiyuk Brings

Let’s get to the numbers, because there’s a lot of romance here and not a lot of data.

With the 49ers, Aiyuk has 294 catches, more than 4,000 yards, and 25 touchdowns in 69 games.

His last healthy season was 2023: 75 catches, 1,342 yards, and seven touchdowns.

Now look at Washington’s receiver room.

Terry McLaurin: out for the season with a torn patellar tendon.
Another young receiver: out for the year.
Dyami Brown: on injured reserve.
Stefon Diggs: 36 catches, 386 yards, six touchdowns.
Antonio Williams: 28 catches, 326 yards.
Treylon Burks: 14 catches, 260 yards.

Add up those three players’ yards, and they don’t reach 1,000.

Aiyuk had 1,342 by himself in his last healthy season.

The Schematic Fit

Schematically, Aiyuk gives Jayden something Washington doesn’t have right now: a receiver who wins with body control and route precision, not just speed.

Aiyuk is one of the best in the league at snapping off intermediate routes: comebacks, 15-yard outs, digs. That’s exactly where Jayden has run out of answers when defenses take away the deep shot.

Remember the stat we covered: 70 percent of Jayden’s throws against the Rams were behind the line of scrimmage or within nine yards. A receiver who can win at 12 to 18 yards consistently is the missing piece.

With Diggs in the slot and Aiyuk outside, defenses can’t roll their safety toward one side anymore.

Part Five: The Part That Scares Me

I’m not going to sell you a perfect picture.

Aiyuk hasn’t played a game since October 20th, 2024. That day, he tore the ACL, MCL, and meniscus in his right knee. That’s more than two years without playing.

Rosenhaus told Russini that Aiyuk is 100 percent healthy, in shape, and working out at full force.

But that’s his agent talking, and that’s an agent’s job. I want to see him run a comeback route against a real NFL corner, not in an Instagram clip.

And there are people who know that locker room and don’t buy it. James Jones, a former NFL receiver and now an analyst, said this week that he doesn’t think we’ll see Aiyuk play football this year.

I’m writing that down, because if he’s right, my whole take today falls apart.

The Contract Fine Print

There’s another thing to consider. Aiyuk is 28. The 49ers voided around 27 million dollars in guarantees in 2025. This isn’t the same player or the same contract people were talking about in 2024.

Whoever claims him on waivers takes on his contract as it is. Peters has to read that fine print with a magnifying glass. Washington does have plenty of cap space, but the details matter.

My Take

I’m saying it on the record, so you can hold me to it.

Peters has to claim him the day he’s released. No hesitation.

And I’ll go further: if Aiyuk is healthy by Christmas, he’s this team’s WR1, ahead of Stefon Diggs.

Diggs is 32 and having a strong season. But a healthy Aiyuk at 28 offers a different level of separation on intermediate routes. And Diggs becomes the best WR2 in the NFC East.

To the People Who Don’t Want Him

To those still saying they don’t want him because of what he said about Jayden’s mom, I’d ask, respectfully: who are we to be more offended than Jayden?

Jayden forgave him. According to Fowler, he was receptive and understanding.

If the quarterback has turned the page, the fan who’s still stuck in July is defending a pride that doesn’t belong to him anymore.

Could I be wrong? Absolutely. San Francisco might put him on the roster out of stubbornness. Peters might let him go. The knee might not hold up.

But if this works, a month from now we’ll be talking about one of the best moves this front office has made since Jayden walked through the door.

Part Six: Black It Out

Let’s shift gears. After all that waiver fine print, I need to talk about something you feel in your chest.

On Wednesday, Jayden posted three words: “Black it out.”

And according to Commanders On SI, Washington will wear its all-black uniform on Monday night against Cincinnati.

The team hadn’t announced it on its own channels at the time of that report, so I’m treating it as one outlet’s report plus Jayden’s post. But when the quarterback posts that, I’m already pulling out my black jersey.

A Quick History

Washington debuted the all-black look in 2022 against Dallas, and part of the fan base reacted badly. People said black has nothing to do with this franchise, that Washington is burgundy and gold, and that it was a marketing uniform.

I understand that argument. Burgundy is the history. It’s Riggins. It’s Art Monk. It’s the Super Bowls.

My Take on the Black Uniforms

I know you’re going to come at me for this, but black is the best uniform this franchise has right now. Period.

Burgundy is for Sunday at 1 p.m. and the history books. Black is for primetime, under the lights, with the whole country watching.

And it isn’t just about looks. When your franchise quarterback posts “Black it out” five days before a Monday night game against the MVP favorite, that’s a message for the building. That’s Jayden saying this week is different. That’s the kind of leadership I care about way more than any press conference.

The One Thing That Bugs Me

The black uniform can’t become an excuse. If Washington loses by twenty on Monday, the jersey isn’t to blame. And the same people calling it beautiful today will call it cursed.

Part Seven: Jonathan Allen Comes Home

On the other side of the line on Monday night, wearing Cincinnati’s colors, will be Jonathan Allen.

If you’re new, here’s who he is. Allen was Washington’s first-round pick in 2017. He grew up in Ashburn and played at Stone Bridge High School, minutes from where this team practices. He made two Pro Bowls in a Washington uniform and played eight seasons here.

Through the worst years of this franchise, under the worst ownership, Allen always showed up.

In October 2024, he tore his pectoral muscle. In March 2025, Washington released him. He spent a year in Minnesota, and in March of this year, he signed with Cincinnati for two seasons.

When he signed, he said he really believed that team was going somewhere. And at seven-and-two, the Bengals have proven him right.

This week, when asked about returning to Northwest Stadium, Allen said it’s obviously going to be emotional come game time.

My Take on Allen

I’m going to say two things that sound like they contradict each other.

First: Jonathan Allen deserves a standing ovation from the DMV when they announce his name. No debate. Anybody who boos him didn’t understand what this franchise was between 2017 and 2024. Allen was one of the few who stayed and put his body on the line when everything was falling apart.

Second, and this is the spicy one: Peters was right to let him go. Allen was coming off the pec injury, he had an expensive contract, and Peters was rebuilding the defensive line around a different vision. It was a cold decision. It hurt at the time. But it was the right call.

Both things can be true at the same time. You love him. You applaud him. And on Monday, you want Jayden to run right past him and make him feel like this isn’t his house anymore, because for four quarters, it isn’t.

And if Allen gets a sack against Washington on Monday, half the stadium will probably clap without thinking about it. Me included. Don’t judge me.

Part Eight: Vegas Has Spoken

If Jayden says “Black it out,” Vegas answers with numbers.

On Sunday, Fox had the Bengals favored by four and a half, with a total of 52.5. According to SI’s betting coverage using FanDuel numbers, it’s now Bengals by five and a half, with a total of 53.5. Commanders Wire reported that some books even opened at six and a half.

On the moneyline, Washington is around plus-225. Bet $100, and you’d win $225 in profit if Washington wins.

The books don’t agree on the exact number, but they agree on one thing: nobody has Washington close.

The Experts Too

And the experts don’t either. According to Commanders Wire, all ten NFL.com analysts picked Cincinnati. All ten. One of them predicted 63–21.

Sixty-three. That’s not a prediction. That’s disrespect.

Why Vegas Is Burying Washington

They have their reasons.

Joe Burrow is the MVP favorite, at around even money. He was named AFC Offensive Player of the Week. He’s completing 74.7 percent of his passes, the best in the league. He has 27 touchdown passes, also the best in the league. And he’s averaging 310 passing yards per game.

And then there’s Ja’Marr Chase, against a Washington secondary that gave up fourteen catches and 150 yards to Puka Nacua, and over 100 yards to Isaiah Likely.

But Vegas Forgot 2024

Here’s what I think Vegas forgot.

Back in September 2024, Washington went into Cincinnati on Monday Night Football with a rookie quarterback and won 38–33. Jayden Daniels completed 21 of 23 passes that night. It was one of the games that announced him to the league.

Burrow against Jayden. Two former LSU quarterbacks. Two Heisman winners from Baton Rouge. And the last time they met on Monday night, Jayden won.

What Washington Needs Monday

One: Jacory Croskey-Merritt. He’s expected back from injured reserve. A real run game keeps Burrow on the sideline.

Two: the takeaway streak. Washington has forced a turnover in every game since Week 3. Against Burrow, it may need two.

Three: a plan for Ja’Marr Chase. No more letting one receiver beat you fourteen times.

Four: the aggressive Jayden from the start. Not just in the final two minutes.

Five: Nick Allegretti at center. Clean snaps and clean protection calls, like against the Giants.
