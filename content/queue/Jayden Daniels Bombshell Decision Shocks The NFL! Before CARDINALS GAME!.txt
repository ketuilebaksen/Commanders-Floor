Six weeks ago, the Washington Commanders were 1–4–1, heading into their bye week, and written off by much of the NFL after a heartbreaking overtime tie against the Giants and a primetime road loss in San Francisco.

Now it's Thanksgiving week, and Dan Quinn's team has won three of its last four games, climbing to 4–5–1. The latest was a thrilling 22–20 walk-off win over Joe Burrow and the Bengals on Monday Night Football.

And this week, Jayden Daniels finally opened up about Brandon Aiyuk, the receiver who attacked him online over the summer and who's now joining Washington's practice squad.

What Jayden said tells you a lot about the kind of leader Washington has at quarterback.

Welcome to Commanders Floor. Hail to the Commanders. Let's go.

Part One: Jayden on Aiyuk

Let's start with the quote everybody's talking about.

Brandon Aiyuk signed with Washington's practice squad this week. He reunites with Jayden, his former teammate at Arizona State, where they played together in 2019 before Jayden transferred to LSU.

The question everyone wanted answered was how Jayden felt, given what happened this summer. In July, Aiyuk went after Jayden on social media, and in one post, he brought up Jayden's mom.

What Jayden Said

Here's how Jayden explained working things out.

He said you don't know what someone else is going through, and Aiyuk didn't know what Jayden was going through at that time either.

Then he went back to the beginning. Aiyuk, he said, was someone who looked out for him when he was 18 years old, who helped him when he first arrived at Arizona State.

So Jayden said he knows Aiyuk's character and the kind of person he is. And he said Aiyuk was man enough to settle it, set their differences aside, and move forward.

Earlier this week, according to ESPN's John Keim, Jayden also said he's a grown man, Aiyuk is a grown man, and things happen, and he called Aiyuk a brother.

Why This Matters

Think about what Jayden did there.

He could've held a grudge. Nobody would have blamed him. Fans were still angry about what was said about his mom.

Instead, he chose grace. He remembered the person who looked out for him as a college freshman, and he gave him credit for reaching out to fix things.

And remember, according to ESPN's Jeremy Fowler, it was Aiyuk who made the first call, about a month ago, and Jayden was receptive and understanding.

That's a franchise quarterback putting the team first. If Jayden had said, "I don't want him here," Adam Peters would never have signed Aiyuk. Instead, Jayden opened the door.

The Reality Check

But let's keep expectations grounded.

According to Keim, Quinn said it'll take a lot of work for Aiyuk to be ready to impact the team, and there's no timetable for him to play.

Aiyuk hasn't played since tearing his ACL, MCL, and meniscus in October 2024. He's on the practice squad for a reason. Don't expect him on the field Sunday in Arizona.

The Lottery Ticket

One analyst on the source program put it well: this is a very low-cost investment. Pay him practice-squad money and see what he's got.

He compared it to buying a stock like Netflix or Uber when it was trading at two dollars a share. You might hit something.

And that's the right way to think about it. The downside is tiny. The upside is a former second-team All-Pro who, at his best in 2023, had 75 catches and 1,342 yards.

Part Two: The Drive

Now let's talk about what Jayden did on Monday night, because it was the signature moment of his young career so far.

The Setup

With less than a minute left, Bengals kicker Evan McPherson drilled a 56-yard field goal to give Cincinnati a 20–19 lead, with 46 seconds remaining according to the Bengals' official site.

Washington took over with its back against the wall and no timeouts. Earlier this season, late-game clock management had haunted this offense. Remember the Rams game, with the final play ending in a failed lateral at the Rams' 31.

The Two Throws

This time, Jayden was calm.

According to the source, he hit rookie Antonio Williams on a 22-yard strike over the middle, then followed it immediately with a 42-yard catch-and-run to Williams.

According to the AP recap on ESPN, Washington went 64 yards in 34 seconds.

Then, with Washington's offensive linemen sprinting downfield to get set, Jayden spiked the ball to stop the clock, and the source notes the offense executed its clock-stopping spikes cleanly.

The Kick

Then rookie kicker Drew Stevens drilled a 26-yard field goal as time expired. It was his fifth field goal of the night.

Washington 22, Cincinnati 20.

Jayden's Night

According to the source, Jayden finished 23 of 35 for 289 yards with no turnovers, and added 55 rushing yards, including a career-long 47-yard scramble.

And according to the source, Antonio Williams finished with nine catches for 122 yards.

What Jayden Said About Pressure

After the game, Jayden explained why he thrives with his back against the wall.

He said he personally lives for high-pressure moments, when you can go out there and make your mark on the game. It's not about stats, he said, it's about wins and losses.

And he said that having the ball with 40-something seconds left, going down the field and setting up the game-winning kick, means a lot to him.

Part Three: Quinn and the Cape

Dan Quinn also talked about his quarterback after the game.

He said it's been cool, over their three years together, to watch Jayden develop not just as a player but as a man in difficult circumstances.

He talked about Jayden knowing when to put the cape on. Not on second-and-1 in the second quarter, Quinn said, but in the fourth quarter, when the game is on the line. That's when Quinn feels Jayden fully express who he is.

Quinn said Jayden doesn't really surprise him anymore, but in those moments, he looks as cool as he is, because he really is.

The Antonio Williams Factor

One of the analysts on the source program made a great observation.

During the game, you could see Stefon Diggs waving his hand at times, asking for the ball. And that's understandable. Diggs is a veteran star who wants the ball in big moments.

But with the game on the line, Jayden went to the rookie. Two throws to Williams, and Washington was in field goal range.

And another analyst made the point: with McLaurin out for the season, Washington found a young receiver who can really play. In his words, Williams is going to be one heck of a player.

Remember, before this game, Quinn publicly said he wanted to get Williams more involved, including on early downs. And on Monday night, Williams was the guy.

Part Four: Why Washington Feels Different

The source program spent a lot of time on how Washington's vibe has changed.

One analyst put it perfectly: that's how crazy this league is. A few weeks ago, people were calling Washington dysfunctional, asking what in the world it was doing in end-of-game situations. And now, after a couple of wins, you can see the team believes in itself again.

The Run Game

Part of it is the run game.

Jacory Croskey-Merritt returned from injured reserve and scored a second-quarter touchdown. According to the source, Washington ran for 139 yards.

And here's why it works so well with Jayden: when he's on the field, defenses have to respect the threat of him keeping the ball and running around the corner for a big gain. That opens running lanes for Croskey-Merritt and Austin Ekeler.

The Defense

The other part is the defense.

According to the source, Daronte Jones's defense pressured Joe Burrow all night, especially in the first-half rain, and held the Bengals to just 12 rushing yards.

And according to the source, Washington outgained Cincinnati 428 total yards to 312.

The Turnover Margin

And remember what Quinn said after the game: the turnover margin was zero. Washington's offense protected the ball, and Jayden didn't turn it over once.

Against a quarterback like Burrow, giving away possessions is how you lose. Washington didn't.

Part Five: The NFC East Race

Now, the big question: can Washington actually win the division?

The Standings

Washington is 4–5–1, two games behind the 6–3–1 Giants. Dallas is 5–6.

And Washington holds a key tiebreaker edge over the Giants: a 1–0–1 head-to-head record, after the tie at home and the win at MetLife Stadium.

What the Pundits Said

One analyst on the source program, who said he's a Giants fan, admitted he's more scared of Washington than of Dallas.

He thinks Washington is a more complete football team, and that Dallas has a tougher schedule. In his words, Washington is dangerous right now.

Another analyst said Washington's win over Cincinnati was far more impressive than Dallas beating Tennessee. The problem, he noted, is that Washington still has to jump both the Cowboys and the Giants to win the division.

The Remaining Schedule

Here's what's left:

at Tennessee
vs. Houston
vs. Atlanta

at Jacksonville
vs. Dallas

The next two games, at Arizona and at Tennessee, are the kind Washington has to win if it's serious about this race.

Part Six: The Arizona Test

So what does Washington need on Sunday in Arizona?

One: avoid the letdown. After two emotional wins over the Giants and Bengals, this is a classic trap game. Arizona went 3–14 in 2025 and has a first-year coaching staff.

Two: handle Josh Sweat. The Cardinals' edge rusher had 12 sacks and four forced fumbles in 2025. He's been slowed this season by a knee issue, but he's still dangerous.

Three: fix the snaps. Quinn admitted this week that Lucas Patrick's shotgun snaps against Cincinnati were inconsistent in location and speed. That has to be cleaned up.

Four: keep feeding Antonio Williams. He's earned it.

Five: keep running the ball. With Croskey-Merritt back, the run game gives the offense balance.

Six: protect the secondary. Amik Robertson didn't practice early this week with an ankle injury. If he can't play, Washington's depth gets tested again.

Part Seven: Jayden, Almost Perfect

One analyst on the source program had a funny take. He said Jayden Daniels is almost a perfect quarterback, and that his only complaint is the injuries around him at receiver.

And that's the point. Jayden has been doing this without Terry McLaurin, without Dyami Brown, with a rookie receiver emerging, and with an offensive line that keeps changing.

Imagine what this offense looks like if Aiyuk gets healthy and up to speed, alongside Diggs and Williams.

That's why the Aiyuk signing matters, even if he doesn't play this year. It's about building weapons around a quarterback who keeps proving he's the real thing.

Part Eight: The Bigger Picture

Let me be clear about one thing: Washington isn't fixed.

The record is still under .500. The fourth-down conversion rate is down to 38 percent from 87 percent in 2024. The third-down offense still needs work, as Quinn admitted himself. And the center snaps are a real issue.

But for the first time this season, Washington is winning the close games. Quinn said it himself: through the first nine games, Washington was 2–3–1 in one-score games. In the last two weeks, it's 2–0.

That's the margin changing.
