The Washington Commanders beat the Eagles, and within hours, phones around the NFL started ringing like a fire alarm.

The losing streak is over. The “season is dead” talk got a little quieter. And suddenly, the biggest name in Washington isn’t a player. It’s the general manager.

Adam Peters has been working the phones, and not casually. Before the Eagles game even kicked off, he went on his weekly radio spot and said something that should have had every fan in the DMV sitting up straight: his staff has made a ton of calls, he’s personally talked to a ton of general managers, and Washington has talked to all of the other 31 teams.

Every single one.

So what is Peters really shopping for? Who’s calling about our guys? And what happened to the Daron Payne trade everybody expected?

Welcome to Commanders Floor. Hail to the Commanders. Let’s go.

Part One: A General Manager Who Dials the Phone

Every team calls every team around the deadline. That’s normal.

But the way Peters said it sounded different. It didn’t sound like a man waiting by the phone. It sounded like a man dialing it.

Washington isn’t just taking calls. It’s starting them, poking around and asking what a team would want for this player or what it would take for that pick. That’s a general manager shopping.

And here’s the part that made the fan base do a double take: Peters talked like a buyer. He said there are only a handful of times a year when you can make real moves, and this is one of them. If something comes up that can help the team, you want to take advantage of it.

Notice what the radio hosts didn’t ask him. Nobody said the word “seller.” Nobody asked if he was tearing it down.

They already knew the answer. Adam Peters is not a tanking general manager. Even if this team were winless, he wouldn’t be selling the farm for scraps. That’s just who he is.

Part Two: The Market Is Already Moving

Why does this matter right now? Because the deadline market has already started to move, and it tells you exactly who’s playing what game.

The Titans shipped star defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons to the Chicago Bears for a package built around two first-round picks. Two firsts. The Bears looked at a five-and-two record and their roster, and went all in.

The Colts sent All-Pro guard Quenton Nelson to the Seattle Seahawks for a third and a fifth, and DeForest Buckner to San Francisco for a fourth.

Sellers are selling, buyers are buying, and everybody is choosing a lane.

Why Sellers Want 2027 Picks So Badly

The sellers have a very good reason to chase picks right now: the 2027 draft class is being talked about as something special, and not just at the top.

Think about the college landscape today. Extra eligibility, the transfer portal, players staying for fifth and sixth years. That means a pile of players who normally would have turned pro earlier are all hitting the same draft. Scouts are saying the depth is absurd.

Players who would have gone in the second round in a normal year could slide into the third. Guys who would have been seventh-rounders could go undrafted.

So when the Titans stack picks in that draft, it isn’t just rebuilding. It’s shopping at the sale of the century.

And remember where that draft is being held: Washington, D.C., on the National Mall.

Part Three: The Window Peters Is Hunting

Peters is trying to take advantage of exactly that dynamic.

In Week 1, every team thinks it can win the Super Bowl, so nobody sells anything. By early November, reality hits. Some teams know they’re contenders and want to add. Others know the season is cooked, and suddenly the players they wouldn’t touch in September are available for the right price.

That’s the window, and that’s what Peters is hunting.

Washington has one very obvious hole. Terry McLaurin is out for the year, and he was the top receiver.

Peters was asked point-blank whether he’s interested in adding another receiver. He could have shut it down. He could have said, “We love our guys. We believe in Treylon Burks. We believe in the rest of the room. Next question.”

He didn’t. He said they’re looking at everything, at every position, both present and future.

Maybe that’s coach-speak. Or maybe it’s a little Bat-Signal flashing over Washington, telling every team with a spare receiver that the Commanders are open for business.

Part Four: The Brian Thomas Jr. Pursuit

That brings us to the name that keeps coming up, and it’s a big one: Brian Thomas Jr.

This is a fascinating story. Two years ago, Thomas was a Pro Bowl rookie, a first-round pick who torched the league with 1,282 yards and ten touchdowns. Then a new coaching staff arrived in Jacksonville, and he fell off the face of the earth. He’s been benched in games and has gone missing from the offense.

Here’s the detail that tells you everything. Before the Jaguars played the Colts this weekend, Jacksonville put out a promotional graphic featuring eight skill players. Brian Thomas Jr. wasn’t one of them.

The Jaguars went out and beat the Colts 42–7 without making him a focal point at all.

He’s only 24 years old, not even in his prime yet, and he’s being treated like an afterthought.

The Market Is Heating Up

So it’s no surprise the trade chatter has exploded. Reports say several teams believe the Jaguars could move him. The Chiefs and Steelers are in the mix, and after the Eagles game came word that Washington is interested as well.

Even more interesting, there’s been a shift in tone from Jacksonville. For a year and a half, the message was that the Jaguars weren’t answering calls. Now there’s a crack in the armor.

Going from zero percent willing to one percent willing might not sound like much, but in trade-rumor math, that’s a landslide.

The AFC Twist That Helps Washington

Here’s the twist that works in Washington’s favor.

The Chiefs and the Steelers are AFC contenders, and the Jaguars play in the AFC. So Jacksonville has to ask itself an uncomfortable question: do we really want to hand a talented young receiver to a team we might face in January?

An NFC team like Washington doesn’t carry that baggage. If the offers are anywhere close, Washington could have a real edge.

The Bidding War Math

There’s another wrinkle in how the suitors line up.

The Steelers reportedly like Colts receiver Josh Downs even more than Thomas, and Downs has requested a trade. The Chiefs reportedly see Downs as a backup plan if they can’t land Thomas.

And Washington? Washington only wants Thomas.

That makes this a bidding war with a lot of moving pieces, and the more teams pile in, the higher the price goes. At some point, the question isn’t whether Thomas is good. It’s whether he’s good at that price. If the cost climbs too high, Peters has to know when to walk away.

My Take on BTJ

This is the one buy that makes sense.

Not a 30-something veteran for a king’s ransom. Not a desperate Super Bowl push for a team still finding itself. A young, talented receiver with team control into the future, a chance to fill the hole Terry left, and a kid who may just need a fresh start with a quarterback who believes in him.

Jayden Daniels already threw him seventeen touchdowns at LSU. That’s a move for 2027 and beyond.

Honestly, I think Thomas gets traded before the deadline. I just don’t know if it’s to us.

Part Five: Who’s Calling About Our Guys?

Now let’s look at the other side of the ledger, because Washington is getting calls too.

K’Lavon Chaisson is the surprise name.

After his big performance against Philadelphia, a report surfaced that the New England Patriots are looking to buy, want edge-rusher help, and that a reunion with Chaisson has been floated as a possibility.

Think about how wild that is. The Patriots let him walk in free agency. Washington signed him to a one-year, twelve-million-dollar deal after the best season of his career. Now New England might be willing to give up draft picks to get back a player it let leave for nothing.

We’ve heard this story before. The Stefon Diggs rumors popped up weeks ago with the same plot: sign a guy, flip him to the team that let him go, and collect a pick on the way out. If Peters pulls that off with Chaisson, it’s less football strategy and more hustle.

But Should He Sell?

On the field, Chaisson was a problem for the Eagles: one sack, three quarterback hits, a tackle for loss, and constant disruption.

Pro Football Focus gave him a middling overall grade for the game, but his pass-rush grade was a strong 79.1. The week before against the 49ers, PFF gave him a sky-high overall grade built mostly on coverage and run defense.

Two games, two completely different grades. That tells you not to lean too hard on any one grading system. But it also tells you the guy can play.

The real question is the future. Chaisson is on an expiring deal. Trade him, and you create an even bigger hole at edge rusher heading into 2027. Remember the list of expiring deals on this defensive front: Dorance Armstrong, Charles Omenihu, Deatrich Wise, Javontae Jean-Baptiste, and Chaisson.

So here’s where I land, and it’s consistent with what I’ve told you all along:

If a team offers a fourth-round pick or better, you take it. That’s the market for expiring pass rushers, and it’s real value.

If a team calls with a fifth- or sixth-round offer, you keep him. You might even try to extend him. If he keeps playing like this, he’ll ask for real money in the offseason, and you’ll have the inside track.

A bargain-bin trade makes no sense for a team that already has needs everywhere.

Part Six: The Daron Payne Story That Stings

This one stings the most.

It was reported that the Giants had serious interest in Daron Payne before he got hurt. Not casual interest. Serious interest.

And there’s a funny twist. Josh Taylor, a writer who covers Washington, called it a week earlier. He said he was about eighty percent sure Payne was headed to the Giants. People laughed at him. Then a top national insider confirmed the Giants’ interest on air.

Now Payne is on injured reserve. His contract is about to run out, and the third-round pick that might have been on the table is gone. No contender trades a meaningful pick for an injured rental.

If he walks in free agency, Washington gets no trade return. At best, there’s a compensatory pick years down the road, depending on the league formula and how many free agents Washington signs. That’s far from a sure thing.

Meanwhile, Charles Omenihu, Tim Settle, and the rest of the interior rotation stepped up and played well against the Eagles without him.

So Was Peters Too Greedy?

That’s what fans are asking.

Maybe there was a third- or fourth-round offer sitting there, and maybe Peters could have taken it. He didn’t, and now the window is closed with Payne on IR.

I’ll own my part too. On this channel, I flipped back and forth on Payne. First I said sell during the bye for a second. Then, when the market said a conditional fourth, I said keep him. Then I said sell for a Day 2 pick. In the end, the injury made the decision for everybody.

The lesson is the one I keep repeating: the price only drops from here. When you have an expiring asset and a real offer, waiting is a bet. Sometimes the bet loses.

Get well soon, Daron. You’ve given this franchise a lot.

Part Seven: The Marcus Mariota Situation

Then there’s the strangest rumor of the week: Marcus Mariota.

Baltimore’s season just took a hit. Lamar Jackson is having thumb surgery and heading to injured reserve with what could be a season-ending injury. His backup, Tyler Huntley, also got hurt, with a suspected turf-toe injury. The Ravens had to put in their emergency quarterback.

Suddenly, a playoff-caliber team in the AFC needs a quarterback, and one of the most experienced backups in the league sits on Washington’s roster.

Mariota is a former starter and former second overall pick. Earlier this season, when Jayden Daniels was out with his elbow, Mariota stepped in. Remember the game against the Raiders when he threw the game-clinching touchdown? He knows how to manage a game.

So the Mariota-to-Baltimore idea makes sense on paper for the Ravens. The question is whether it makes sense for Washington.

My Take on Mariota

I’d be very careful here, and I’ll tell you why.

Jayden Daniels has already missed time this season with an elbow injury. He’s been on the injury report with that elbow for weeks, even while practicing fully. And Jayden plays a style that exposes him to hits. He runs, he scrambles, he extends plays.

If Washington trades Mariota and Jayden goes down in November, the season is over. Not “in trouble,” over.

I said it before the Eagles game: if this team has to go to the backup, the season gets very hard. But having a backup who has won games in this offense is very different from having no proven backup at all.

So here’s my line: if Baltimore offers a real pick, a third or better, and Washington has a credible plan at backup, you listen. For anything less, Mariota stays. A late-round pick isn’t worth risking the whole season on one hit to your franchise quarterback.

Part Eight: The Rams Game Decides the Lane

All of this comes back to the next game: the Rams on November 8th, two days before the deadline.

Beat the Rams, and Washington is three-four-and-one, two games behind the Giants with nine to play, in a division where Dallas is three-and-five and Philly is two-and-six. Peters’s buyer talk suddenly looks very smart, and a move for BTJ becomes even more urgent.

Lose to the Rams, and Washington is two-five-and-one. The buyer talk gets quieter, Chaisson becomes much more sellable, and the focus shifts to the 2027 draft on the National Mall.

Either way, Peters has done the groundwork. He’s talked to all 31 teams. He knows the prices. He knows who’s buying and who’s selling. When the Rams game ends, he’ll know which lane he’s in.

Part Nine: My Full Deadline Plan

Let me lay out the whole Commanders Floor plan for the final week.

Buy:

Brian Thomas Jr. Young, under team control, already connected to Jayden. Pay a reasonable price, but walk away if the bidding war gets out of hand.

Sell, only for real value:

K’Lavon Chaisson, for a fourth or better. Below that, keep him and try to extend him.
Marcus Mariota, only for a third or better, and only if there’s a credible backup plan. Otherwise, he stays as Jayden’s insurance.

Accept:

Daron Payne is on IR, and the window to trade him has closed. It’s a missed opportunity, and the lesson is to sell expiring assets while they still have value.

Never:

Spend future picks on 30-something rentals. Every buy has to be for Jayden’s window, 2027 and beyond.

Final Word From Commanders Floor

Let me wrap it up.

Adam Peters has talked to all 31 other teams, and he’s talking like a buyer, not a seller. He’s looking at every position, present and future.

The market is moving. Simmons went for two firsts, Nelson for a third and a fifth, Buckner for a fourth. And the 2027 draft is so deep that sellers want those picks more than ever.

Brian Thomas Jr. is the one buy that makes sense, and Washington has a real edge as an NFC team. But the Chiefs and Steelers are in the mix, and the price could climb.

K’Lavon Chaisson has a potential reunion suitor in New England. Sell for a fourth or better. Otherwise, keep him.

Daron Payne is on IR, and the Giants’ serious interest came too late. That window has closed.

Marcus Mariota could interest a Ravens team in a quarterback crisis, but Washington has to protect itself behind Jayden.
