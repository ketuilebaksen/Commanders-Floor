Tyreek Hill. The Cheetah. One of the fastest players in NFL history is still sitting on the open market in November. No team. No contract. No snaps in more than a year.

And Washington just beat the Eagles 27–10, is two games out of first place in the NFC East, and is still missing its number-one receiver, Terry McLaurin, for the rest of the season.

So every Commanders fan is asking the same question again: should Adam Peters call Drew Rosenhaus? And could Tyreek Hill be the piece that turns this season around?

I’m going to give you the dream, and then the latest reality, including a surprise move by Hill that changes everything. Welcome to Commanders Floor. Hail to the Commanders. Let’s go.

Part One: Why the Hill Conversation Is Back

Let’s start with why this name keeps coming back to Washington.

On Monday night in San Francisco a couple of weeks ago, Terry McLaurin went down on Washington’s second possession. He had already caught his only target, a 27-yard gain. Then, running a route with nobody touching him, he grabbed his knee and couldn’t get to the sideline on his own. The trainers put an air cast on his left leg, and he was carted to the locker room.

The next day, Dan Quinn confirmed the worst: a torn patellar tendon in his left knee, out for the season. Quinn said he was gutted for him, and so are we.

Terry finished with fifteen catches, 230 yards, and a touchdown in five games, and he was still the team’s leading receiver. He’s under contract through 2028, but he’ll be coming back from a serious knee injury, and nobody knows what this receiver room will look like by then.

This team has been snake-bitten all year: Laremy Tunsil out since training camp with a torn triceps, Jayden Daniels missing games with an elbow injury, and now Terry.

What’s Left in the Room

Stefon Diggs is now the clear number one. Behind him are Antonio Williams, the third-round rookie whom Adam Peters says has a sky-high ceiling, Treylon Burks, Dyami Brown, Luke McCaffrey, and Chig Okonkwo at tight end.

Williams and Burks have flashed. But against the Eagles, Jayden completed just sixteen passes. Defenses can sit on Diggs and dare somebody else to beat them. That’s the problem in one sentence.

So the question comes up again: does Washington go get a weapon?

Part Two: Enter the Cheetah

Tyreek Hill is the most famous free agent in football.

Miami released him in February. He’d dislocated his left knee and torn multiple ligaments, including his ACL, in Week 4 of 2025. He’s now in his early thirties.

His résumé is ridiculous. He’s an eight-time Pro Bowler. In 2023, he led the entire league with 1,799 receiving yards and thirteen touchdowns. Since entering the league in 2017, he has more than 11,300 receiving yards, the most of anyone in that span, and 83 touchdown catches. That’s a potential Hall of Fame résumé.

And Washington’s name has come up before. Back in June, ESPN’s Aaron Schatz picked signing Tyreek Hill as the one move Washington should make before the season, with a big caveat: only if he’s healthy.

Part Three: The Dream Scenario

Let me paint the picture, because I’m a fan too, and this part gives me chills.

It’s November 8th. The Rams come to town. Jayden Daniels drops back, and lined up outside in burgundy and gold is the fastest man on the field.

Before the snap, the Rams’ safety is already backing up, because nobody in this league plays Tyreek Hill one-on-one without help over the top. So the middle of the field opens up. Diggs gets one-on-one on a slant. Antonio Williams runs his in-routes into wide-open space. Okonkwo finds room up the seam. The running game sees lighter boxes.

And then, once a game, Jayden rolls out of the pocket, buys a second, and lets one fly. Hill runs under it, and it’s gone.

Remember what Hill did to this franchise in December 2023: two long touchdowns, a 78-yarder and a 60-yarder, and Washington had no answer. Imagine that speed on our side for once.

Now look at the standings. Washington just beat the Eagles. The Giants lead the NFC East at four-two-and-one. Dallas blew a fourteen-point lead to Arizona and is three-and-five. Philly is two-and-six, without Jalen Hurts. Washington is two-four-and-one, two games out with ten to play.

A team that suddenly has Diggs and Hill, with Jayden throwing to them and a defense that just held Philly scoreless for three quarters, could get hot. And you know what happens in this league when a team with a dynamic quarterback gets hot in November.

That’s the dream, fam. I won’t pretend it doesn’t exist.

But you came to Commanders Floor for the truth. So here’s the latest reality, and it’s changed since we last talked about this.

Part Four: The Reality Check, November Edition

There are now five big obstacles between that dream and reality.

One: he’s still not on a roster, and the timeline has slipped.

Rosenhaus said back in early October that Hill could sign with a team within days. His target was an October return. October is over. Hill still isn’t on a roster, and there haven’t been reports of any workouts with NFL teams.

Two: the surprise move.

This is the new part. According to reports this week, Hill traveled to Germany to receive treatment on his injured knee as he tries to speed up his recovery. That’s a serious step, and it tells you the knee isn’t where his camp hoped it would be by November.

Think about what that means. Hill’s game is built on speed. If he’s still seeking specialized treatment more than a year after the injury, doubts about whether he can play at all in 2026 are growing.

Back in July, he admitted in a workout video that he had no power in his left leg. The later clips looked more explosive. But a trip to Germany in November isn’t what a player who’s ready to sign next week usually does.

Three: even the Chiefs moved on.

Kansas City, Hill’s first team and the one that knew him best, was the favorite all along. Now ESPN’s Dan Graziano says the Chiefs have plenty of receivers. Rashee Rice is back, and Kansas City is looking at the trade market instead. Reports say a Hill reunion with the Chiefs looks dead.

If the team that knows him best isn’t calling, that tells you a lot.

Four: the money and the “contender” question.

According to Jonathan Jones of The Athletic, one reason Hill is still unsigned is that he doesn’t want a veteran-minimum deal. Rosenhaus has said Hill wants to play only for a contender.

Would Washington qualify? After the Eagles win, maybe it’s closer than it was. But it’s still a two-four-and-one team.

Five, and this is the most serious one: the legal situation.

On October 13th, a six-person jury in Miami found Hill liable on two of ten counts in the civil case brought by his estranged wife, connected to incidents in January 2024 and April 2025, and awarded her $500,000 in damages. Hill denied the physical abuse allegations.

The NFL has confirmed the matter is still under review under its personal conduct policy, and that policy doesn’t require criminal charges for a suspension. Some analysts think a suspension is more likely to come before he signs anywhere.

So any team that signs Hill right now could be signing a player who doesn’t play for weeks, and taking on all the questions that come with it.

Part Five: The Detail Nobody Is Talking About

Here’s something important that almost nobody mentions in the Hill conversation.

Tyreek Hill is a free agent, so he isn’t tied to the November 10th trade deadline.

The deadline applies to trades, not free-agent signings. Washington could sign Hill at any point this season, whenever his knee and his league situation are cleared.

That changes the strategy completely.

Washington doesn’t have to choose between Hill and a deadline trade. Peters can make his trade-deadline moves now, for players like Brian Thomas Jr., and then keep Hill as a possible late-season option if everything falls into place.

That’s the smart way to think about it. Hill isn’t a deadline decision. He’s a wait-and-see decision.

Part Six: Washington Has the Tools, If It Wants Them

Let’s be fair to the dream for a second. If Hill does get cleared, Washington has the tools.

The cap space: according to Over the Cap, Washington has about 25.5 million dollars in cap space, the sixth-most in the league. If Hill wants more than the minimum, Washington can afford it.

The general manager: Peters said on the radio that Washington has talked to all 31 other teams, and that he’s looking at every position, present and future. He’s clearly not afraid to make calls.

The need: McLaurin is out, and Jayden needs a vertical threat.

The quarterback: a young star who can extend plays and throw deep is exactly the kind of quarterback a speed receiver wants.

So if Hill is healthy and cleared, Washington isn’t a crazy landing spot. It just isn’t the first one, and right now it isn’t reported to be any.

Part Seven: My Take

Here’s where Commanders Floor stands on Tyreek Hill in November.

Keep the phone line open, but don’t build the plan around him.

Doing your homework is the job. Washington should stay in contact with Rosenhaus, monitor the knee, and track the NFL’s review. If Hill is cleared medically and by the league, a short, incentive-based deal could make sense, especially for a team chasing a playoff spot.

But the real move this week is Brian Thomas Jr.

Look at the contrast:

Hill: in his early thirties, coming off a reconstructed knee, getting treatment in Germany, facing a possible suspension, and wanting more than the minimum.
BTJ: 24 years old, healthy, on a fully guaranteed rookie deal through 2027 with an option for 2028, a Pro Bowler as a rookie, and seventeen college touchdowns with Jayden Daniels. And Washington is reportedly interested.

Is it even close? For the future, BTJ is the better bet by a mile.

My plan: go get BTJ before the deadline. Then, if Hill is cleared later in the season and still unsigned, take a low-risk swing on a short deal. That way you get the young cornerstone and keep the dream alive.

What I don’t want is Washington skipping BTJ because it’s waiting on Hill, and then ending up with neither.

Part Eight: Can Hill Make Washington a Champion?

I know that’s what a lot of you want me to say.

Here’s the honest answer, because I’d rather lose a view than lose your trust: no single signing makes this team a champion this year. Not with a two-four-and-one record, a defense that’s still near the bottom of the league in points allowed despite the Eagles game, and a team still finding its identity.

What Hill could do, if everything breaks right, is give this offense a weapon that changes how every defense plays Jayden. He could give the season a spark. And in a division this broken, a spark can go a long way.

So the dream isn’t “Hill makes Washington a champion.” The dream is “Hill helps Washington steal a division nobody wants to win.”

And that dream depends on a knee in Germany and a decision at the league office.

Part Nine: What to Watch Next

Here’s what to keep an eye on over the next few weeks.

First: Hill’s knee. Any report of him working out for NFL teams would be a big signal that he’s close.

Second: the NFL’s decision. Until the league finishes its review, every team will be hesitant.

Third: the deadline. Whatever Washington does with BTJ by November 10th affects how much it needs Hill later.

Fourth: the Rams game. Beat the Rams on November 8th, and Washington is three-four-and-one, making a late-season Hill signing look a lot more like a contender’s move.

Terry McLaurin is out for the season, and Washington still needs a vertical weapon for Jayden Daniels.

Tyreek Hill is still the dream: an eight-time Pro Bowler with a Hall of Fame résumé, available for nothing but money. But as of now, he’s unsigned. His October return target came and went. He’s receiving knee treatment in Germany. The Chiefs have moved on. And the NFL’s review is still open.
