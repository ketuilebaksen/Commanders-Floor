Two days of practice. Zero snaps.

That’s what Trent McDuffie has given the Los Angeles Rams this week. And if you’re a Commanders fan, that line on the injury report might be the most interesting sentence you read before kickoff.

McDuffie didn’t practice on Wednesday. He didn’t practice on Thursday. He’s still working his way back from a back injury he suffered in Week 8. Next to him in the training room is safety Kam Curl, who also missed both days with a foot injury. And cornerback Jaylen Watson is out for the season after shoulder surgery.

Put those names together, and you get the reason I believe Washington has its best chance in a long time to keep this season alive.

Let me be clear from the start: we don’t cheer for injuries, and we don’t enjoy them. But they’re the story of this game, and they work in Washington’s favor.

That’s dose number one. Dose number two is the Myles Garrett situation. Dose number three is a game plan that fits this matchup perfectly.

Welcome to Commanders Floor. Hail to the Commanders. Let’s go.

Part One: The Stakes

First, let’s talk about what’s on the line today.

If Washington beats the Rams, it will have its first two-game winning streak with Jayden Daniels since the 2024 playoffs.

Think about that. That’s how long it’s been since this team won back-to-back games. Since the run to the NFC Championship Game.

And after today, Washington has a short week before playing the Giants on Thursday, with a chance to close the gap at the top of the division. The Giants lead the NFC East at four-two-and-one. Dallas is three-and-five. Philly is two-and-six. A win today puts Washington at three-four-and-one, with a direct shot at the division leader four days later.

That’s how fast the balance of power can change in this league.

But we can’t look that far ahead. We have to focus on today.

Part Two: Another “Must-Have” Moment

I’ve said several times this season that a game was the biggest opportunity to save the year.

I said it about the Colts game. I said it after the win over Seattle kept the hopes alive. We felt that way before the Giants game too. Then came a step back in San Francisco. And last week, Washington absolutely had to beat the Eagles, and it did, scoring 27 straight points in a 27–10 win.

Now here we are again, with another opportunity.

The Rams Carry a Massive Brand

Let’s be honest about who Washington is playing today.

The Rams were among the preseason Super Bowl favorites. They’re stacked with talent, and they have arguably the best head coach in the NFL in Sean McVay.

And yet I think they’re leaving the door wide open for Jayden Daniels to have a phenomenal game. If he does, Washington has a really good chance, because with number five, anything is possible.

I don’t just want Jayden to walk through that door. I want him to kick it down, storm through, rip this win away, and send this team on a run toward the playoffs.

Part Three: Dose Number One. The McDuffie Factor.

The details start with Trent McDuffie.

The Rams traded with the Chiefs for him this offseason. In fact, they ended up with both of Kansas City’s starting corners: McDuffie came via trade, and Jaylen Watson came via free agency.

In my view, McDuffie has been the best player on the Rams this year. Matthew Stafford hasn’t played up to his usual standard. Puka Nacua has been really good, but not quite at his ceiling, and he missed time earlier in the season. Myles Garrett has barely played.

McDuffie is on a very short list of the best cornerbacks in the NFL. And he didn’t practice on back-to-back days.

That’s huge news.

Will He Play?

The Rams are on West Coast time, so their final injury designations come late on Friday. But Friday is usually when teams start ruling players out, and missing the first two practices of the week is not normally how a player gets on the field on Sunday.

Even if McDuffie suits up, there are real questions. Is he at higher risk of re-injury? Is he limited? Is he himself? All of that is on the table.

That would be a massive blow for the Rams, especially with Watson already out for the season. Los Angeles could be without both of its starting corners.

The Emmanuel Forbes Storyline

If McDuffie can’t go, one of those spots goes to Emmanuel Forbes, Washington’s former first-round pick.

Forbes struggled in Washington, and the Commanders eventually released him. He had plenty to say about Washington after he left. Today, he gets a chance to back it up. Or he looks like the Emmanuel Forbes Washington remembers.

If McDuffie doesn’t play, Forbes and Josh Wallace would be the starting corners. That’s a big downgrade, from one of the best corners in football to two players who have mostly been backups.

And Then There’s Kam Curl

Add Kam Curl, the Rams’ safety, who also missed Wednesday and Thursday with a foot injury. Is he going to play? And if he does, will he be the best version of himself?

Think about this logically. If McDuffie, Curl, and Watson all miss this game, the Rams are without most of their starting secondary.

Part Four: Why That Matters So Much

It’s not just about talent. It’s about communication.

These would be players who aren’t used to being on the field when the bullets are flying. If they’ve gotten snaps, it’s usually as fill-ins who rotate back out. They’re not used to playing starter-level snaps, and now they’d have to take the lead on communication.

There’s a bigger idea behind this. Football coaches talk about trust in a secondary, and trust comes from repetition. Defensive backs learn where their teammates will be, how a safety will react to motion, and when a corner needs help over the top. That knowledge doesn’t come from film alone. It comes from playing together.

A secondary full of fill-ins has to build that trust in a week, then hold it together in a game against a quarterback who can improvise. That’s exactly the kind of mismatch that creates open receivers. And open receivers are what Jayden needs.

The Rams’ Defense Lives on Its Back End

Here’s why this hits the Rams so hard.

The Rams have been one of the best defenses in the NFL against the pass. And they’ve done it without much pressure. They rank only 21st in the league in pressure generated.

They’ve survived without a dominant pass rush because their secondary has been so good. Take away that secondary, and everything gets complicated.

That’s why I keep coming back to Jayden Daniels. Commanders fans already go into games expecting that he has to be really good for Washington to win. How about a game where he could be facing backups in the secondary?

We just saw this team play an injury-depleted opponent last week. We weren’t even convinced by the performance, and Washington still scored 27 unanswered points and won by 17.

If there’s ever a time to play the Rams, it’s right now.

Part Five: Dose Number Two. The Myles Garrett Situation.

It’s not just the secondary. There’s an update on Myles Garrett too.

In typical Commanders fashion, it seems like whenever a star player has been out and is about to face Washington, he suddenly comes back.

On Monday, the Rams opened the 21-day return window for Garrett. That window opens when a team is ready to start working a player back from injured reserve. Once it opens, the team has 21 days to activate him to the 53-man roster. It doesn’t mean he has to play immediately or in the next game, just within that period.

On Wednesday and Thursday, Garrett was limited in practice. That’s more than McDuffie and Curl, who didn’t practice at all.

Will Garrett Actually Play?

When a player comes back from injured reserve, you normally want to see him stack a full week of practice.

Washington is going through the same thing with Johnny Newton. When Dan Quinn was asked on Wednesday whether Newton would play this weekend, he basically said the team wants to see him get through a full week of practice before considering it.

The Rams have to be just as careful. I know they feel they haven’t gotten what they wanted from the Garrett trade, because he’s barely played this season.

Let’s look at it fairly. In Week 1, he didn’t record a single stat. He clearly wasn’t himself, dealt with knee issues, went on injured reserve, and had to rehab. When you bring him back and he can’t make it through a full day of practice, two days in a row, do you throw him out there right away?

If you’re the Rams, you have to think long term. It was a win-now trade, but you can’t be so desperate that you rush him back.

If Garrett does play today, it almost has to be on a snap count. There’s no way a player limited in his first week back plays a full 50 or 60 snaps. And whatever version of Garrett shows up, it won’t be the best version. He’s barely played since the end of 2025, and he won’t be game-sharp.

Part Six: Being Fair to the Rams

I want to be fair to Los Angeles here.

The Rams are good enough to play through injuries. One reason they’re good is that they’ve been disciplined on the back end, even without a strong pass rush.

That’s actually the whole point of my argument. They’ve survived by being sound in coverage. Take away that soundness, and the whole structure changes. A pass rush ranked 21st in pressure can’t carry a defense on its own, and without a reliable secondary, the Rams are leaning on a plan that hasn’t been tested.

And remember what else is going on with the Rams. They haven’t had a bye yet, and this is a lot of football on banged-up bodies. Their tight ends, Colby Parkinson and Terrance Ferguson, have been banged up, and so has offensive lineman Steve Avila. Matthew Stafford, who’s 38, is playing through a right elbow issue and has thrown eight interceptions. The betting line has already moved a full point toward Washington, from five and a half to four and a half.

Part Seven: Dose Number Three. The Game Plan.

Here’s the part I’m most excited about: the game plan.

Even if McDuffie and Curl play, we know Watson is out. And we know the Rams’ tendencies: they play a lot of zone coverage and a lot of two-high safety shells.

Zone is good against mobile quarterbacks, and I expect the Rams to be heavy in zone like they’ve been all year, because they don’t want Jayden to take over the game with his legs.

That’s exactly why I’m looking at Antonio Williams and Stefon Diggs.

Why Williams and Diggs Are the Keys

Whether or not the Rams are playing backups in the secondary, I don’t trust their linebackers in coverage.

In zone, defenders are watching the quarterback instead of the receivers. That gives Williams and Diggs a chance to win over the middle and find the soft spots in the zone. They can also win quickly off the line.

I fell in love with the Antonio Williams tape I broke down on this channel this week: the routes he runs, the way he gets open against good defenders. He can get open against this Rams defense, and we know Diggs can too.

The Quick Game Beats the Two-High Shell

Washington hasn’t been able to run the ball very effectively. And a two-high shell is designed to encourage you to run. With Jacory Croskey-Merritt and Jeremy McNichols on injured reserve, that’s not Washington’s strength.

So I think Washington counters with the quick game: short throws over the middle, five to seven yards, to Williams and Diggs.

Think about how the quick game matches up against zone. A two-high shell tries to keep everything in front of it and prevent big plays. The price is that the short and intermediate areas can be open. A quarterback who gets the ball out quickly to receivers who win early takes advantage of that.

That plan doesn’t need a strong running game, and it doesn’t need a lot of time in the pocket. It needs accurate throws and receivers who know how to find space. Williams and Diggs both fit that description, and that’s why I expect so much of the offense to run through them today.

And here’s the bonus: if the Rams’ backup defensive backs start creeping up to stop the short game, that’s when you take the shot. Treylon Burks just beat a first-team All-Pro corner for 54 yards last week. Against backups, that shot is there.

Part Eight: The Defensive Side

Quickly, on the other side of the ball.

Washington’s run defense hasn’t allowed a 100-yard rusher all season. That’s the matchup to watch against Kyren Williams and Blake Corum, who power the Rams’ offense.

It’ll be tested, because Daron Payne and Javon Kinlaw are both likely out for the season. Shy Tuttle, Tim Settle, and newly promoted Ricky Barber have to hold the middle.

If they do, the Rams become one-dimensional, and Washington can make Stafford throw into a secondary that now has Trey Amos, Amik Robertson, and Rasul Douglas, with Steve Wilks on the sideline helping Daronte Jones.

And with Odafe Oweh at four sacks, K’Lavon Chaisson at three and a half, and Sonny Styles and Frankie Luvu blitzing, this defense can get to a 38-year-old quarterback with an elbow injury.

Part Nine: Why This Game Matters for the Deadline

One more thing: this is the last game before Tuesday’s trade deadline.

Win today, and Washington is three-four-and-one. Adam Peters, who has talked to all 31 teams and sounds like a buyer, gets the green light. Ian Rapoport says Washington could land Colts receiver Josh Downs for a third- or fourth-round pick. Brian Thomas Jr. is still out there. Even a cheap running back like Tony Pollard could be in play.

Lose, and the conversation flips to selling.

So today isn’t just about one win. It decides what this roster looks like for the rest of the season.

Part Ten: My Prediction

I think Washington wins this game.

Here’s why:

The Rams’ secondary is banged up. McDuffie and Curl missed practice, Watson is out for the season, and Emmanuel Forbes could be starting.
Garrett, if he plays, will be rusty and on a snap count.
The Rams’ pass rush is only 21st in pressure. Jayden should have time.
The Rams will play zone, and Washington’s quick game with Williams and Diggs is built to beat it.
Washington’s run defense hasn’t allowed a 100-yard rusher all season.
Momentum: Washington is coming off its best win of the year, and the line is moving its way.

My keys to winning:

One: quick game early. Williams and Diggs, five to seven yards, over and over.

Two: take the shot when the backups creep up. Burks deep.

Three: hold the middle against Kyren Williams and Corum.

Four: win the turnover battle against Stafford.

Five: start fast. No falling behind 10–0 this time.

Dose number one: the Rams’ secondary is in trouble. Trent McDuffie, maybe their best player, missed practice Wednesday and Thursday with a back injury. Kam Curl missed both days with a foot injury. Jaylen Watson is out for the season. Emmanuel Forbes and Josh Wallace could be the starting corners.
