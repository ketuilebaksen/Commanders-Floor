How do you score 40 points and still lose?

That’s the question the Dallas Cowboys are asking themselves after falling to the San Francisco 49ers. And it’s also why the Washington Commanders just got some of the best news of their season.

Washington is coming off a Thursday night win over the Giants, and somehow things got even better. Three developments landed in a short window, and one of them could matter more to Washington’s season than people realize.

This hasn’t been the year anyone expected. But a team that has been searching for a lift now has a few real reasons to believe.

Welcome to Commanders Floor. Hail to the Commanders. Let’s go through them one by one.

Part One: Good News Number One. Croskey-Merritt Is Coming Back.

Let’s start with the offense, because the first piece of good news is the expected return of running back Jacory Croskey-Merritt for Monday night’s game against the Cincinnati Bengals.

Adam Peters has indicated he’s on track to return, and John Keim of ESPN has also said Washington expects him back.

Nothing is guaranteed until he’s officially activated. But the timing makes sense. He’s missed four games since landing on injured reserve before the 49ers game, which is the minimum required, and this backfield desperately needs another option.

Setting Expectations

Let me be clear about expectations, though.

A seventh-round pick usually isn’t the answer to a team’s biggest problem, and I’m not claiming he is. But when a backfield isn’t producing, a player with a different skill set, even a modest one, can change how a defense has to prepare.

Variety has value, and this group hasn’t had much of it.

His Numbers

He’s not an established star. He’s a seventh-round pick with plenty left to prove.

But in his time on the field this season, Croskey-Merritt has 239 rushing yards, about 3.6 yards per carry, and two rushing touchdowns, which still leads Washington’s running backs. He also has five catches for 40 yards.

Those numbers aren’t spectacular. But he’s shown he can break off the occasional explosive run, and that’s something this group badly needs.

What Washington Has Been Getting Without Him

That matters because of what we’ve seen from Rachaad White and Austin Ekeler.

I don’t think they’re terrible players. But Washington isn’t getting consistent production from this backfield.

White has 275 yards on 65 carries, about 4.2 yards per attempt. Ekeler has 109 yards on 33 carries.

Too often, the backs barely combine for twenty carries, and drives stall. Washington doesn’t have a workhorse like Buffalo’s James Cook, but that’s no reason to abandon the run. When the passing game stalls, your quarterback needs help.

The Depth Problem

The depth makes it even more frustrating.

Jeremy McNichols is out for the season. Robert Henry Jr. left for Seattle for a while before Washington brought him back, and he got just two carries against the Giants. Craig Reynolds isn’t going to transform this offense either.

So yes, Croskey-Merritt’s return matters. I want to see him get real opportunities, find a rhythm, and have this team commit to the run.

Long term, Washington needs a true starting running back, and Croskey-Merritt is probably best as a second or third option. But for now, he’s the best chance this offense has to get something going on the ground.

Why the Run Game Matters for Jayden

Here’s why that’s so important.

Before the Giants game, Jayden Daniels had been about as passive as he’d ever been: 70 percent of his throws against the Rams went behind the line of scrimmage or within nine yards. Part of the reason is that defenses haven’t had to respect Washington’s run game. They sit back in two-high safety looks, take away the deep ball, and dare Washington to run.

If Croskey-Merritt can force safeties to creep up even a little, the deep shots open up. Play-action starts to work. And the aggressive Jayden we saw at the end of the Giants game has more room to operate.

Part Two: Good News Number Two. The Defense’s New Identity.

While everybody talks about the offense’s problems, the second piece of good news is the defense.

Have you looked at the turnover numbers?

Washington already has fourteen takeaways, compared with just ten in all of last season, and we’re only around the halfway point. Since Week 3, this group has found a way to take the ball away every week, and it’s becoming this defense’s identity.

I don’t think that’s luck anymore.

Takeaways Are a Habit

A defensive identity is built on habits. Forcing turnovers isn’t a one-game thing. It’s the result of practicing how to strip the ball, pursuing in groups, and reading quarterbacks.

When a team has more takeaways halfway through the season than it had in all of last year, that’s a sign those habits have taken hold.

What It Looked Like Against the Giants

Look at what happened against the Giants.

Frankie Luvu forced a fumble from Malik Nabers that Jeremy Reaves recovered.

Even special teams got involved, forcing another fumble, although New York recovered that one.

That’s the effort I love seeing. Talent isn’t everything. Sometimes it’s eleven players knowing their assignments and refusing to quit.

Washington is flying to the football, playing physical, and making opposing offenses uncomfortable.

The Run Defense

That physical play shows up against the run too.

Washington is allowing just under four yards per carry, and no individual running back has reached 100 yards against it this season. Not Christian McCaffrey. Not Jonathan Taylor.

Players like Blake Corum and Cam Skattebo have had productive moments, so it’s not a brick wall. But giving up the occasional good run isn’t the same as getting dominated on the ground. The front seven deserves real credit for that, especially with Daron Payne and Javon Kinlaw out.

The Honest Reading of the Numbers

These numbers deserve a clear reading.

18 sacks and six interceptions show a defense that makes plays.

But 17 passing touchdowns allowed and more than 2,300 net passing yards show a defense that also gives up a lot.

A defense can be aggressive and still be vulnerable. The next step is closing that gap without losing the aggression that creates the takeaways.

That’s why I wouldn’t call this an elite defense yet, especially when opponents find chances downfield, like Isaiah Likely’s 66-yard touchdown on Thursday. Still, I see real progress.

Daronte Jones Deserves Credit

I give Daronte Jones credit, because I’m starting to see what he wants to build.

What I like most about Jones is the aggression.

Remember the late pressure packages against the Giants? Washington sent heavy pressure, forced J.J. McCarthy into a tough spot, and Rasul Douglas came away with the interception that sealed the game.

That’s exactly what I want from a defense protecting a close lead. Don’t sit back and hope for a mistake. Create one.

Douglas also made a big interception to help close out the Eagles game. Those are winning plays, and Washington needs more of them.

Steve Wilks’s Influence

I also like that Steve Wilks is involved in the defensive planning. He’s a part-time assistant, so I won’t overstate his role. But his experience can help, and since he started appearing on the sideline on game days, Washington has gone two-and-one.

Peters’s Defensive Additions

People have criticized Adam Peters for a lot of decisions this season, and on this channel, we’ve been among them. But his defensive additions deserve appreciation.

Nick Cross and Amik Robertson have helped establish a different identity. Odafe Oweh has five sacks despite some inconsistency.

Not every acquisition works. But I respect building a faster, more physical defense.

Where This Defense Can Go

Honestly, I believe this system can get even better with the right additions. Washington still needs help at free safety, and Jones needs time to fully develop his scheme.

What Brian Flores has built in Minnesota shows why personnel and identity matter. Washington isn’t at that level, but I can see a path there.

My concern is whether this staff gets enough time to finish what it started. That’s something we’ll revisit when the season ends.

Part Three: Good News Number Three. The Cowboys Lost Again.

Now the third piece of good news, and this one could be huge for the playoff picture.

The Dallas Cowboys just lost 46–40 to the 49ers.

Dak Prescott and that offense put up enough points to win most games. But the defense couldn’t get it done.

San Francisco piled up 584 yards. Dallas fell behind 38–19, rallied, and still took another loss.

It was a game that showed just how much Dallas needs defensive help.

Credit and Concern

Dallas showed fight, and I give the offense credit for the comeback. But allowing 46 points is a massive problem. Defensive coordinator Christian Parker has to fix that defense, because you can’t keep asking Prescott to win shootouts every week.

McCaffrey and the 49ers moved the ball at will.

And here’s what stands out to me: Washington held that same 49ers team to 23 points. That doesn’t make Washington better in every area. But it says something about how these two defenses are performing.

What a 40-Point Loss Tells You

There’s another angle I find interesting.

When a team scores 40 and loses, it tells you the offense isn’t the problem. It points to the defense, the special teams, or the way the game was managed.

For Washington, which is trying to catch the Cowboys, that’s a reason to feel a little better. A team with a defensive problem is vulnerable, no matter how good its quarterback is.

Why This Loss Matters

Dallas now sits at four-and-six.

The Cowboys still have some manageable games left, so a loss to a tough opponent like this one is exactly what Washington needed to see. Every loss they take keeps the division race open.

And after watching that Dallas defense struggle, I feel better about Washington’s chances in the Week 18 meeting at Northwest Stadium. I want to see Jayden get another shot at Dallas while he’s healthy. Remember, Jayden was hurt in the first meeting. If Washington protects him and finds some balance on offense, there should be chances against that secondary.

Part Four: The NFC East Picture

Put the Cowboys’ loss together with Washington’s win over the Giants, and the NFC East suddenly looks a lot more interesting.

Here’s where things stand:

Giants: five-three-and-one.
Cowboys: four-and-six.
Commanders: three-five-and-one.
Eagles: two-and-seven.

Washington is two games behind the Giants, with a win and a tie against them this season. That means Washington holds the head-to-head tiebreaker if the two teams finish tied.

The Playoff Odds

Washington’s record isn’t what anybody wanted. But before Thursday’s win, the Commanders were near the bottom of the NFC standings. Afterward, they climbed to 11th.

Some playoff projections now put Washington’s chances around 20 percent. ESPN’s model is lower, at around 14 percent. Either way, that guarantees nothing, but it’s enough to keep paying attention, especially given how many close games this team has played.

Why the Record Is Misleading

That point about circumstances is important.

A record like three-five-and-one can hide the fact that a team has been competitive in most of its games. If your quarterback is hurt in one game, or you’re down to your third-string quarterback in another, a loss tells you less about the quality of the team than it seems.

Think about the missed opportunities. Washington had chances against the Eagles in Week 1, the Cowboys, the Colts, and the Rams, plus that earlier tie with the Giants.

I’m not counting those as wins. But Washington has been competitive against good opponents. Against Indianapolis, it was dealing with a third-string quarterback situation. Against Dallas, Jayden was hurt.

The record tells you what happened. It doesn’t always tell you why.

Part Five: The Schedule Helps

The remaining schedule helps too.

The Giants still face: the Jaguars, Colts, 49ers, Seahawks, Browns, Lions, Cowboys, and Eagles. That’s a tough road, with several playoff-caliber teams.

Washington still faces: Cincinnati, at Arizona, at Tennessee, Houston, Atlanta, at Minnesota, at Jacksonville, and Dallas.

I don’t expect Washington to win all of those. But if this team is serious about the playoffs, it has to beat teams like Arizona and Tennessee and compete for wins against Houston and Atlanta.

You can’t keep dropping winnable games and expect other teams to rescue your season.

What I Want to See

I’m not asking for a miracle. A competitive game against a strong offense, a functioning run game, and a couple of takeaways would be a great sign.

If Washington can do those things, the next few games become a lot more interesting, and those playoff projections could move even more.

Part Six: The Bengals on Monday Night

That brings us to Monday night against the Bengals, and it’ll be fascinating.

Joe Burrow against Jayden Daniels. Two quarterbacks with LSU ties, both former Heisman winners from Baton Rouge.

And Cincinnati’s offense can put real pressure on Washington’s secondary. Ja’Marr Chase is a player this defense absolutely has to account for, especially after what Puka Nacua did to Washington with fourteen catches for 150 yards, and what other elite receivers have managed against this group this year.

Is It a Must-Win?

I don’t consider it a must-win, since it’s an AFC opponent and division opportunities remain.

What I want is a competitive performance that shows the Giants win wasn’t just one good night.

The Jayden Factor

Speaking of Jayden, his response against the Giants was one of the most encouraging parts of that win.

He threw two interceptions, his first two of the season after 207 straight clean passes, and the game could have slipped away. But he stayed involved, kept competing, and delivered the game-winning touchdown to Stefon Diggs with 57 seconds left.

That’s what you want from a franchise quarterback. Nobody expects perfection every week. But you want a quarterback who recovers from his mistakes instead of letting them define the game.

The Challenges Ahead

Challenges remain, of course, especially Minnesota later in the season. The Vikings’ defense is aggressive, and Dallas Turner has been creating problems for quarterbacks. Jacksonville on the road won’t be easy either.

But one game at a time. First, the Bengals.

Part Seven: What to Watch Monday Night

Here’s what I’ll be watching against Cincinnati.

One: Croskey-Merritt’s workload. Does Washington give him fifteen or more carries, or does it fall back into the twenty-total-carries trap?

Two: the takeaway streak. Can the defense force a turnover against Burrow, one of the smartest quarterbacks in the league?

Three: Ja’Marr Chase. Does Daronte Jones have a real plan for him, or does Washington get burned the way it did by Puka Nacua?

Four: the aggressive Jayden. Does the offense let him take deep shots early in the game, not just in the final two minutes?

Five: Antonio Williams. After playing 86 percent of the snaps against the Giants and getting only two targets, does he finally get the ball more?

Final Word From Commanders Floor

Let me put it all together.

Good news number one: Jacory Croskey-Merritt is expected back from injured reserve against the Bengals. He’s not a star, but he still leads Washington’s running backs with 239 yards and two touchdowns, and this backfield badly needs him.

Good news number two: the defense has a real identity. Fourteen takeaways already, after just ten in all of last season. A turnover every week since Week 3. No 100-yard rusher allowed all year. The pass defense still has work to do, but Daronte Jones’s aggressive scheme is clearly taking shape.

Good news number three: the Cowboys lost 46–40 to the 49ers and fell to four-and-six. Combined with Washington’s win over the Giants, the NFC East race is wide open.

The big picture: Washington is three-five-and-one, two games behind the Giants with the head-to-head edge, an easier remaining schedule, and playoff odds somewhere between 14 and 20 percent.
